# A Promise, Not a *Finding*

For forty-nine issues we have signed off *Love you down to your star stuff*. The grammar of that phrase has been doing the hardest work on this site, and nobody had said so. Here is what it claims, what it carefully does not, and why that is the stronger position.

---

Everything here is signed with one phrase. It is on **thirty-five** of these pages, in five registers, and it is the closest thing this project has to a thesis.

Love You Down To Your Star Stuff.

Read it as a sentence rather than a logo and two things come into view. Both are load-bearing. Neither is decoration.

**The subject is us.** *\[We\] love you down to your star stuff.* There is no cosmic agent anywhere in it — the loving is being done by particular people, on purpose, and the phrase has never pretended otherwise.

**And “down to” measures reach, not cause.** It says how far the love goes, not why it is given. Not *because you are star stuff, therefore loved* — but *loved all the way down, star stuff included*. The star stuff is the **extent** of the claim, not its warrant.

That distinction is the whole of this page, and it matters because there is a sentence one short step away that gets both parts wrong. **We wrote it once.**

## The sentence we wrote once

At the foot of [Bone Song](https://starstuff.earth/bone-song-zine.html#spread-6) — Zine No. 1, the first thing this project ever made — the colophon ends like this:

“You are made of star stuff. The universe loves you for it. So do we.”

Bone Song · Zine No. 1 · colophon

The middle sentence is the naturalistic fallacy stated flatly. **The universe** is the subject, doing the loving; **for it** makes the star stuff the reason. Both moves are exactly what the rest of this page argues against.

And then the next three words take it back. ***So do we.***

We are pointing at that line rather than quietly deleting it, for two reasons. A site that keeps a [public error log](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) does not get to tidy its own first page and pretend it was always clean. And more to the point: **those three words are this entire essay, written before any of the rest of it.** The repair was already in the building. What follows is just the argument for why it was needed.

A correction this page exists downstream of

[No. 49](https://starstuff.earth/one-atom-of-justice-zine.html) quoted that Bone Song line, called it **“our masthead,”** and dropped the *So do we* — then argued against what was left. Neither half of that was right. It is a colophon on one zine, not a masthead; the masthead tagline is *Cosmic Connections*; and the sentence had already corrected itself.

**Ryan caught it** on 15 August 2026, which is why this essay is built on the phrase we actually use. The zine is corrected and the error is logged in the [changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) rather than absorbed quietly.

So: the strongest objection to a claim like ours, in its strongest form. It comes from a comic fantasy novel, which is where we found it, so the credit goes above the argument rather than in a footnote below it.

## The objection, in its strongest form

In *Hogfather*, Death is explaining to his granddaughter Susan why humans need to believe in things like the Hogfather. She pushes back — they are not the same as the big things, she says — and he answers with the hardest sentence anyone has aimed at this website.

YOU THINK SO? THEN TAKE THE UNIVERSE AND GRIND IT DOWN TO THE FINEST POWDER AND SIEVE IT THROUGH THE FINEST SIEVE AND THEN *SHOW* ME ONE ATOM OF JUSTICE, ONE MOLECULE OF MERCY. AND YET—Death waved a hand. AND YET YOU ACT AS IF THERE IS SOME IDEAL ORDER IN THE WORLD, AS IF THERE IS SOME . . . SOME *RIGHTNESS* IN THE UNIVERSE BY WHICH IT MAY BE JUDGED.Death, in Terry Pratchett's *Hogfather* (1996), verbatim — the small capitals and both italics are the novel's own

He is right. That is the whole reason this page exists.

## There is no atom of justice in the powder

Grind this entire site down. Every citation, every field guide, every carefully sourced figure, every abundance table. Sieve it. **There is no molecule of *and therefore you belong* in what comes through.**

This is the naturalistic fallacy, and we have named it in the house rules from the beginning: you cannot get an *ought* out of an *is*. But naming a fallacy is not the same as understanding why it would be fatal *here specifically*, and that part is worth spelling out, because it is not an abstraction for us.

**The same move, run the other way, is how eugenics argued.** *Nature does X, therefore we should do X* is a machine that will manufacture a welcome or an extermination depending only on which fact you feed it, and the people feeding it have always been able to find a fact. Our [chain collection](https://starstuff.earth/collection-how-we-got-here.html) exists because good physics keeps getting misapplied to human beings by exactly this route.

So the danger is not that the argument is impolite. It is that **it does not hold, and it can be turned around**. And there is a sharper consequence still:

The part that decides it

If our claim that you belong rested on *variation persists in nature*, then a paper finding that variation does **not** persist — in some species, some system, some scale — would revoke it. The belonging would be contingent on the literature holding still.

**A claim that the next paper can take away was never doing the work we needed it to do.** Which is not a reason to abandon it. It is a reason to be honest about what kind of claim it is.

## Which is why the grammar matters

Pratchett's answer arrives a few lines earlier in the same conversation, and it is the one we actually use. The small lies — the Hogfather, the tooth fairy — are *practice*. And what they are practice for is the big ones: **justice, mercy, duty**. Which are also not found in the powder, and which exist anyway, because enough people acted as though they did for long enough. Susan makes our objection almost word for word — people have *got* to believe that, or what is the point — and Death's reply is three words long: *MY POINT EXACTLY.*

NO. YOU NEED TO BELIEVE IN THINGS THAT AREN'T TRUE. HOW ELSE CAN THEY *BECOME*?Death, in Terry Pratchett's *Hogfather* (1996)

*How else can they become.* Not a consolation — **a construction schedule.** Justice is not a thing anyone discovered; it is a thing being built, badly and unevenly and for a long time yet, and belief is the scaffolding you stand on while you build it.

Now put Death's sieve beside our phrase, because they travel in the same direction and make opposite kinds of claim. He grinds the universe *down* to the finest powder and goes looking for justice among the atoms, and finds none, correctly. **Love you down to your star stuff** goes down exactly as far — and does not claim to have *found* anything there. It claims that the loving *reaches* that far.

Down to is not because of. One of those is a discovery, and the other is a distance we are willing to travel.

So the phrase never needed correcting. The love in it was always ours, doing what we do, and the star stuff was always the measure of how far it goes rather than the reason it is given. What was missing was not better wording. It was anybody saying out loud that the wording is *doing* something — and that the sentence we wrote once, in the colophon of No. 1, is what it looks like when the grammar slips.

A finding can be overturned by the next paper. A promise can only be broken by the people who made it.

That is not a weaker position than the one we appeared to be in. It is a much stronger one, and it is also a great deal more demanding — because it means **nobody else is holding it up**. There is no cosmic backstop, no fact in reserve that will do the arguing for us if we stop. The universe is not going to pathologize your variation, and it is not going to welcome you either. It is not that kind of thing. *We* are that kind of thing.

## Every piece here is a ladder

The second rule falls out of the first. If the physics is not carrying the ethics, then the physics has one job — being accurate — and we have to be candid that every piece here *simplifies* it.

There is a proper name for that, and it is not ours. **Jack Cohen** and **Ian Stewart** called it a *lie-to-children*: a simplification that is false, and is nevertheless the only way to get somebody to the next rung. It is what your physics teacher is doing when they tell you electrons orbit the nucleus like little planets.

We do it constantly. *The atoms in your body were forged in dying stars* is a rung — hydrogen is primordial, and a great deal of the rest arrives from stellar winds, neutron-star mergers and cosmic-ray spallation. *Bone is piezoelectric* is a rung, and one we got wrong twice before [correcting it](https://starstuff.earth/hatchery.html): the effect is collagen's, not the mineral's.

So what separates an honest simplification from the thing this site was built to fight? One property, and only one.

*the same simplification, two practices*

| a lie-to-children | a smoothed retelling |
| --- | --- |
| **says it is a rung** | presents itself as the top |
| **names what it left out** | files off the hedges, because the tidy version travels further |
| **survives being climbed past** | collapses the moment somebody checks |

Which is what the house phrase **“a rhyme, not a proof”** has been doing at the foot of piece after piece without ever having a name. It is a rung marker. So is the [wood-wide-web hedge](https://starstuff.earth/mycelium-and-rhizome-zine.html), where the mycorrhizal trade is ours to use and the forest-wide story is not.

**And the ladder has a moral limit as well as an epistemic one**, which is the part that is genuinely ours. [Field Guide No. 10](https://starstuff.earth/turtle-field-guide.html) keeps five *turtles people made* in their own section with no correction table, because a table that fixes a mistaken verdict has no business being run over somebody's cosmology. There the rung stops for a reason that has nothing to do with accuracy. *Some things are not ours to climb past.*

## First Sight and Second Thoughts

The third rule is the method, and we were using it for a year before we found out it had a name — in a children's book about a girl learning to be a witch.

“First Sight and Second Thoughts, that's what a witch had to rely on: First Sight to see what's really there, and Second Thoughts to watch the First Thoughts to check that they were thinking right.”

Terry Pratchett · Wintersmith, 2006

**First Sight is seeing what is actually in front of you** rather than what you expected to be there. It is harder than it sounds, because the expectation arrives first and feels exactly like observation. It is why the [field guides](https://starstuff.earth/collection-field-guides.html) keep arriving, independently, at *there is no standard one of these* — that is what you find when you go and look instead of consulting the type specimen in your head. It is also what our corrections look like: the mantis shrimp's famous colour vision collapses on contact with the literature, and the wood wide web rhymed so beautifully with mutual aid that it got past us in three separate pieces.

**Second Thoughts are the thoughts that watch the first ones.** Not doubt — supervision. And this is the half you can build machinery for, which is the only reason this site has any:

*second thoughts, as installed*

| the first thought | what watches it |
| --- | --- |
| “this quote is obviously by…” | **`FACTCHECK.md`**, and the rule that aggregators are leads and never citations |
| “the page looks fine” | **six checking gates** that measure instead of glancing |
| “we got that right, surely” | a [**changelog that publishes our own errors**](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) |

That last one is the Second Thought that costs something, and it is the reason the changelog is a public page rather than a private log. **Publishing your own corrections is not a virtue display; it is the only way first thoughts ever get supervised where somebody can see it happen.**

The asymmetry matters, though, and it is worth ending this section on: **only the second half can be automated.** No gate will ever tell you that a beautiful pattern is doing your looking for you. First Sight needs a person who is willing to be disappointed by a finding they loved — which is the same person [the previous essay](https://starstuff.earth/who-is-holding-the-candle.html) is about.

## What this licenses, and what it refuses

Every argument on this page has a cheap version that sounds nearly identical, and the cheap versions are common enough that leaving them unnamed would be negligent.

Not:

- **Not permission to believe whatever you like.** The little lies are practice for justice and mercy, not for anything that happens to be comforting. A belief that has to *become* true puts you under an obligation the moment you hold it, and most comfortable beliefs ask nothing of anybody.
- **Not a softening of a single figure.** Everything checkable here is still checked to the same standard, by the same gates, with the same ledger. This page changes what the checking is *for*; it does not change how hard it is done.
- **Not relativism.** “There is no atom of justice” is a claim about where justice is *located* — not a claim that cruelty and care are the same, or that it does not matter which you choose. It matters more, not less, once you know nothing else is holding it up.
- **Not an argument that fiction beats evidence.** The opposite, and emphatically. [This whole collection exists](https://starstuff.earth/collection-how-we-got-here.html) to reclaim science from the things that wear its clothes.
- **Not a demotion of the physics.** The star stuff is true, it is checked, and it is the most beautiful fact we know. It earns its place in the phrase entirely on its own. It simply does not carry the ethics, and it never claimed to.
- **Not a claim that we planned this.** We are not going to pretend the phrase was engineered to dodge a fallacy. It was written as an act of love and the grammar came out right, and we only noticed why it was right in the course of getting something else [wrong](#the-sentence-we-wrote-once). Being correct by instinct still counts, but it is not the same as having reasoned it out, and this page is the reasoning arriving late.
- **Not ours.** Three borrowed ideas hold this page up. Two are Cohen and Stewart's and one is Pratchett's, they are credited above rather than below, and the full account of the borrowing — including two attribution errors that finding it corrected — is in [No. 49](https://starstuff.earth/one-atom-of-justice-zine.html).

## The shorter version

You are made of star stuff. That part we checked, and it is the most beautiful fact we know.

The universe does not love you for it, because the universe is not the kind of thing that loves. **We are.** That is not a discovery being reported to you — it is a thing being done, on purpose, and it will go on being done.

And the phrase we have signed off with since the first zine never claimed otherwise. It has been saying the right thing in the right grammar for forty-nine issues, and all this page does is finally say why.

Love you down to your star stuff. Not *because of* the star stuff. All the way *down to* it.
