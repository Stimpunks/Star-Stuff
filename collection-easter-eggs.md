# Not on the *Front Page*

A Star Stuff collection · eight eggs

Real pieces, made to the same standard as everything else here, deliberately kept off the index. There are two small doors in the site footer, and search will find them. Nothing is hidden from the search index, the sitemap, or the checking tools — only from the front page.

---

These are off the path. Each one is about the site rather than about the world — our numbering, our own mistakes, our furniture — and none of them has a card on the front page, because a listed egg is not off the path.

Off the path is not a lower standard. Same sourcing, same grading, same ledger, same checks. The only thing that moves is the door.

How else to find them, and the two conventions this page breaks on purpose, are below the cards.

## The eggs

Listed here, and nowhere on the front page. The other ways in are below the cards.

### [Out of Order](https://starstuff.earth/out-of-order-zine.html)

Zine No. 37 · Easter Eggs · the gap, annotated

*There was never a No. 37, and that was not an accident.*

*an apology to a number we held and never used — and three famous 37s that are not what you were told*

The one hole in our numbering, filled by a page that admits it does not belong there — and corrects its own premise on spread two.

**Details**

The one hole in our numbering, filled by a page that admits it does not belong there. No. 37 was assigned to a zine on 11 August 2026, held back so a question could go to **Helen Edgar** rather than assumed, and arrived two days later as No. 45 because seven pieces had shipped past it — so the gap was a receipt, not a slip. The zine corrects its own premise on spread two, then takes three famous 37s apart: Wunderlich's **37 °C**, averaged in 1868 from a million armpit readings and now about 0.4 °C out of date; the **37%** of any search you should spend looking and refusing everything; and the number people reach for when told to be random, which is marked *contested* because it comes from an unrefereed survey. Then it refuses to connect them, because a coincidence dressed as a mechanism is the fault this whole site was built to name. Closes on the honest arithmetic — *g*(5) = 37, the third star number, the mirror with 73 — and on a page younger than No. 46 whose own number says otherwise. Nine spreads, seven diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [True Facts About True Facts](https://starstuff.earth/true-facts-zine.html)

Zine No. 47 · Easter Eggs · a credit, paid late

*The animal is never the punchline. That is the part we took.*

*on a debt this collection owes a comedy channel, what we took from it, and the one thing about it nobody checked*

Six zines and three field guides here exist because Ryan watched Ze Frank's *True Facts* — and until this page his name appeared nowhere in the project.

**Details**

Six zines here and three field guides exist because Ryan watched **Ze Frank**'s *True Facts* with a young person in our community whose special interest is sharks — and until this page, his name appeared **nowhere in this project at all**. The debt slipped through because the citation habit points at claims rather than causes. What we actually took is narrower than “it makes science fun”: the comedy lands on the narrator, on human expectation, on language, and never on the organism for being a defective version of something else — *ridiculous is not deficient* — which is the same turn the field guides' reframe tables make from the opposite end, without needing the table. Then his own line, from Pamela Hill Nettleton's *U.S. Catholic* interview: **“flawed and wonderful”** in one breath with no ranking between them. Then the arc that runs against every incentive — a channel whose first outing “included neither truth nor facts” and which went *toward* NOAA archive footage and named researchers. And then the fact-check the title owes: **the origin of *True Facts* exists in three incompatible published versions**, and the dates suggest a 2022 profile confused the original with its own 2013 redo. Closes on why the tone stays where it is — same joke, same goodwill, different room. Eight spreads, six diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set. He has no involvement and we did not ask.

### [Twelve Cosmic Eggs](https://starstuff.earth/hatchery.html)

The Hatchery · Easter Eggs · the specimen sheet

*A drawing is not evidence. The source line under it is.*

*The sheet behind the door.*

Twelve cosmic eggs, named where they appear and then deliberately not drawn — the same line Field Guide No. 10 drew around its five turtles people made.

**Details**

The sheet behind the door. The **cosmic egg** — a universe that was closed and then opened — is one of the most widely repeated images people have, and this page names where it appears (Hiraṇyagarbha in the *Rigveda*, the Ogdoad's at Hermopolis, Ilmatar's in the *Kalevala*, Pangu's, Taʼaroa's, the Dogon Amma) and then draws **none of them** — the same line **Field Guide No. 10** drew around its five *turtles people made*. **A borrowed shape is not a borrowed belief.** The twelve are ours, and each carries one thing we can check: the **leatherback**, which has no bony carapace and dives to 1,280 m on hundreds of irregular ossicles; the **Arrau**, filed as a silent animal until a hydrophone caught 2,122 sounds in eleven types, hatchlings calling from inside the shell and females answering; **collagen**, not hydroxyapatite, as the source of bone's piezoelectricity — the error our own Manifesto made twice; painted turtle hatchlings **supercooled below −10 °C**, where survival turns on what touches them rather than how tough they are; endosymbiosis as an egg that arrived rather than grew; the **double empathy problem** as two jagged edges that don't interlock, with the gap in neither half. And the one on the door has our own argument against it printed underneath: it began as a spiky profile, which **No. 21** refuses as *a better chart is still a chart* — so the drawing keeps the jag and throws away the baseline and the grid. Closes on a dotted, empty twelfth. Unnumbered on purpose.

### [Eight Esmx](https://starstuff.earth/quillery.html)

The Quillery · Easter Eggs · the mascot, drawn for the sky

*A quill is a hair, and what makes it stiff is sulfur made in a dying star.*

*The second drawing sheet, and the first about somebody else's character.*

Eight Esmx the Porkypine — the Stimpunks mascot by Kaya Oldaker, CC BY-SA 4.0 — redrawn in this site's line, each carrying one checkable thing.

**Details**

The second drawing sheet, and the first about somebody else's character. **Esmx the Porkypine** — part pig, part porcupine — is the Stimpunks community mascot, drawn by **Kaya Oldaker** and released **CC BY-SA 4.0** with an explicit invitation to make variants. These are eight of ours, built from scratch in this site's line rather than traced, each carrying one checkable thing. **Helium** was found in the Sun's spectrum in 1868 and not on Earth for another twenty-seven years, which is what Esmx's mane already does — one mass coming apart into separate coloured lines. A quill is a **modified hair**, and its microscopic barbs both raise the force needed to pull it out and *lower* the force needed to push it in (*PNAS*, 2012). Porcupines **cannot throw quills**, a myth assembled backwards from finding a predator studded with them. Spines were arrived at **four separate times** in four mammal lineages that share no spiky ancestor. And the payoff: hard keratin is stiff because of **disulfide bridges**, sulfur is made by oxygen burning in the last stretch of a massive star's life, so the reason a spike stands up is an element assembled before the Sun existed. Then the honest one — the spiky profile that Esmx exists to evoke is a *chart*, which **No. 21** refuses, and the mane is better than the diagram because it has no baseline. Closes on the name: Esmx is **Esme**, for Granny Weatherwax, which puts a Discworld citation in the mascot all along. **And it records a citation it nearly got wrong** — a draft credited the spiky profile to the paper that quotes it most, which is a use of the term rather than where it came from.

### [Ten bows to one scale](https://starstuff.earth/bow-ery.html)

The Bow-ery · Easter Eggs · the third drawing sheet

*A 22° ice halo really is half a rainbow’s radius — and on the other side of the sky.*

*The listicle shows you ten photographs at ten focal lengths. This is the same ten drawn to one scale.*

Ten bows at three units to the degree, every plate centred the same way and carrying the 42° primary as a dashed ghost, so the radii can be compared by eye.

**Details**

The third drawing sheet, and the first built on a *measurement* rather than a motif. Every plate is drawn at **three units to the degree** around a shared centre, and carries the 42° primary as a faint dashed ghost — which is the only way a drawing can tell you that a **22° halo is not a small rainbow** but a different thing at half the radius, made of hexagonal ice rather than water, and *centred on the sun*: to see the ghost on that plate you would have to turn around. It leads with **the one bow [Field Guide No. 20](https://starstuff.earth/rainbows-field-guide.html) has no card for** — the plain rainbow, uncardable in a catalogue that sorts by what changed, because in this one nothing has. Its fact is that the bands are ours: Newton’s optical lectures of **1670–72 name five** colours, and *Opticks* in **1704** prints seven, with orange and indigo added where the half-steps fall in the diatonic scale. **The light has no bands in it at all.** Also here: Alexander’s dark band drawn at its true 7.3°; the supernumeraries geometric optics cannot produce; the circumhorizontal arc that needs the sun above 58° and is impossible north of 55°N, available roughly **140 hours a year in London against 670 in Los Angeles**; the glory, drawn tiny against the ghost and carded as still unexplained; and the third-order bow drawn in the glare that kept it unphotographed until 2011. It closes on the thing a drawing sheet is uniquely placed to admit — **magenta is not in the spectrum**, there is no wavelength that makes it, and it is the colour nine of these ten plates are partly drawn in. *There is no version of the drawings that fixes that.*

### [Ten faint lights, one eye](https://starstuff.earth/glimmery.html)

The Glimmery · Easter Eggs · the fourth drawing sheet

*We tried to draw ten glimmers to one brightness scale. It cannot be done honestly.*

*So the thing held constant on every plate is not the light. It is the eye.*

Ten measured faint lights, each drawn against the same block of rods — the 1942 visual threshold — because the scale we wanted turned out to be unbuildable.

**Details**

The fourth drawing sheet, and **the first whose held constant is an admission of failure**. The plan was the [Bow-ery](https://starstuff.earth/bow-ery.html) move transposed from angle to brightness — ten glimmers on one logarithmic strip, faintness comparable by eye. It collapsed on contact: only four of the ten are *surface brightnesses* at all, and putting a mushroom, a reactor pool and a single photon on the same bar would have meant inventing numbers. So the constant moved from the source to the detector. Every plate carries the same block of ~500 rods from **Hecht, Shlaer & Pirenne (1942)**, of which **5 to 14** must each absorb one quantum before you see anything — and that paper’s real gift is its fifth conclusion: the assumption had been that the stimulus is constant and the organism variable, and *at the threshold it is the stimulus which is variable*. The ten: a **single photon** reported above chance, with the headline **P = 0.0545** printed as-is rather than swapped for the stronger confidence-restricted figure; **airglow**, because ozone and hydrogen make hydroxyl glow at **87 km** all night with no sun on it; the **zodiacal light**, which emits nothing; the **gegenschein** at **1.3×** the ecliptic minimum, off grains darker than asphalt; **noctilucent clouds** at **83 km**, lit by a sun already set; **foxfire**, on a temperature-compensated circadian clock and calling insects where there is no wind to carry spores; a **dinoflagellate** answering a touch in **15 milliseconds**; **Cherenkov blue**, a shock front at **48.6°** that breaks no rule; **fireflies**, who get an **82%** response in step and **3%** out of it; and the **Milky Way**, hidden from more than a third of humanity. **Nothing on the sheet is drawn dim** — faintness on a screen is a lie about a brightness the reader set, and on paper it disappears. It closes by naming the three claims it could not source and left off.

Field Guide No. 13 · Easter Eggs · the watching list

*Watch the video, then read what the literature actually says.*

*There is no standard way to be interested in an animal.*

Sixteen animals worth an evening, each with a True Facts video and field notes taken from the papers underneath it. Companion to No. 47.

**Details**

The companion catalogue to **No. 47**, and the watching list that produced half our animal writing. **Sixteen animals**, each with a *True Facts* video and field notes taken from the papers underneath it. Twelve pair with something already published here and link back — the **mantis shrimp** to our Umwelt zine, where the famous claim collapses (they discriminate wavelengths *worse* than we do); five entries to the nervous systems guide; the **crab** to *Five Times a Crab*, whose real finding is that the shape was lost more often than gained; the **shark** to the skeleton that turns out to have *lost* its bone rather than never grown it. Then four more: the **aye-aye**, whose tapping survived every attempt to explain it as simple echo; the **sea cucumber**, which throws its organs and spends five weeks regrowing them; the **nudibranch**, which eats stinging cells and mounts them, still loaded, on its own back; and the **hedgehog**, here because nobody knows why it foams and because the word *(Redo)* in its title is the exhibit in No. 47's fact-check. The reframe table corrects the popular verdict on the *animal* — never anything in the videos, which is the point of the companion zine — and one entry deliberately has no row aimed at a belief. **Nothing loads until you press play.**

*on a debt to Terry Pratchett, two ideas with the wrong name on them, and the objection our whole tagline has to survive*

A credit to **Terry Pratchett** that turned into three corrections. The visible debt was small and correctly filed — Great A'Tuin in No. 35, the *turtles people made* section of Field Guide No. 10. The real one had been sitting on stimpunks.org since 2024: **First Sight and Second Thoughts**, from *Wintersmith*, which is this project's working method described better than our own documentation manages, and which was never once named as a source. The *Science of Discworld* books alternate — Pratchett's Discworld chapters, **Ian Stewart** and **Jack Cohen**'s science chapters — so *Pan narrans*, the storytelling chimpanzee, sits in the science half, and **lies-to-children is Cohen and Stewart's from 1994**, five years before Discworld carried it anywhere. Narrativium is genuinely his. Aggregators file all of it under the name that sells. The zine then turns lies-to-children on our own simplifications, reads the Industrial Revolution novels as asking who a technology costs, and explains the clacks Overhead and the `X-Clacks-Overhead` header **this site now sends**. It closes on Death's challenge in *Hogfather*, quoted verbatim from the book: there is no atom of justice in the powder, which means our own masthead is **a promise we make, not a fact we found**. How this collection works

## What an egg is here

An easter egg on this site is **not a lower standard**. Every piece filed here carries the same requirements as any other: sources traced to primaries, claims graded, a logged entry in `FACTCHECK.md`, a dated line in the [changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html), and a clean pass through all six checking gates. If a piece could not survive that, it would not be an egg. It would just be sloppy, and being tucked away would make it worse rather than better.

What makes it an egg is only *where the door is*. These pieces are about the site rather than about the world — its numbering, its own mistakes, its furniture. Putting them in the index grid would ask every first-time reader to care about our internals before they had read anything. So they sit one step off the path, for the reader who is already looking closely.

## Two conventions this page breaks, on purpose

This collection is the exception to two rules the rest of the site follows, and the house habit is to say so on the page rather than let it look like an oversight.

- **It has no section on the index.** Every other collection here exists because a section of [the front page](https://starstuff.earth/index.html) needed a home, and owes that section an introduction and a link. This one inverts that: a collection page with nothing pointing at it from the grid. That is the entire mechanism of the thing — if it were listed, it would not be off the path.
- **It was created at one member, below the two-member floor.** That floor is real and it was argued for: [How We Got Here](https://starstuff.earth/collection-how-we-got-here.html) was deliberately deferred at one member and created only when a second piece arrived with the same shape, because [collections here are outputs, not inputs](https://starstuff.earth/cosmic-connections.html) — what earns a page is a form *observed in finished work*, never a folder opened in advance. This page was a folder opened in advance, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. **It reached two members the same day**, which vindicates nothing — the floor exists so that a page has to be *earned* rather than guessed at, and getting the guess right is luck, not method.

The reason we took that trade: an egg needs a home that the [working papers](https://starstuff.earth/collection-notes.html) could not honestly give it, and the alternative was a hand-written exemption inside one of the checking tools — a page the tools were told to stop asking about. **A collection page is visible and a tool exemption is not**, and between a convention bent in public and a gate quietly narrowed, the first is the safer mistake. It is recorded in `DECISIONS.md` with the same reasoning.

## How to find them

Three ways, none of them a puzzle. There is no code to crack here and nothing is gated on cleverness — an easter egg that punishes people for not spotting it is just a page you hid.

- **The doors.** Two, in the footer of [the front page](https://starstuff.earth/index.html). A bare **37** sits among the ordinary links where it reads as boilerplate, and goes straight to [No. 37](https://starstuff.earth/out-of-order-zine.html). The other is a drawn **cosmic egg** at the bottom of the page, on its own, which comes here — it replaced an egg emoji on 14 August 2026, because at 14px wedged between two credits nobody read the emoji as a door. *Off the path is not the same as too small to see.* The drawing is one of twelve; the rest are in [The Hatchery](https://starstuff.earth/hatchery.html). Both doors carry text labels, so a screen reader announces them as plainly as it announces *About* or *Search* — an egg only sighted readers can find is not an egg, it is an exclusion.
- **Search.** Every egg is fully indexed, down to the individual spread — so [searching](https://starstuff.earth/search.html) for what a piece is actually about will surface it like anything else. The collection *name* is stripped as chrome, the same as every other collection name, so “easter eggs” is not itself a search term.
- **This page.** Which you have, so that worked.

Not:

**Not a lower bar.** Same sourcing, same grading, same ledger, same gates. The only difference is the location of the link.

**Not hidden from the tooling.** Eggs are in `sitemap.xml`, in `search-index.json`, and inside every check. A page the gates cannot see is a page that rots, and hiding one from them would be the actual failure this collection is arranged to avoid.

**Not a place to put weak work.** If something is not good enough for the index it is not good enough for here either. This is not the drawer.

**Not a licence to backfill the numbering.** [No. 37](https://starstuff.earth/out-of-order-zine.html) occupies one deliberate gap and spends a spread marking itself for it. Every other gap in the sequence stays empty, because the numbers mean *when* and an archive that gets edited stops being evidence.

## The Turtle Moves

*Great A'Tuin, the four elephants, and the Disc · drawn for this page*

> “Mountains rise and fall, and under them the Turtle swims onward. Men live and die, and the Turtle Moves. Empires grow and crumble, and the Turtle Moves. Gods come and go, and still the Turtle Moves. The Turtle *Moves*.”
>
> *Didactylos, in Terry Pratchett's Small Gods (1992)*

*Small Gods* is a book about an institution that kept every instrument of belief and mislaid the thing itself — a church with an enormous apparatus and a god reduced to a tortoise nobody can hear. The Turtle in it is not a doctrine. It is **the thing that goes on being the case whether or not anyone believes it**, and the heresy that gets people tortured is the flat refusal to stop saying so.

Asked whether it is *really* true, Didactylos does not argue. He says the Turtle exists, that this will go on happening either way, and then the three words the whole book has been walking toward: **“It is real.”** That is why the passage sits at the foot of this collection rather than anywhere else. [The eggs are the pages about our own furniture](https://starstuff.earth/collection-easter-eggs.html#what-an-egg-is-here) — our numbering, our mistakes, where our method came from — and this is the sentence underneath all of it. [No. 49](https://starstuff.earth/one-atom-of-justice-zine.html) spends eleven spreads conceding that the universe contains no atom of justice and that our masthead is a promise rather than a finding. The Turtle is the other half of that, and the half we never get to choose: **some things are simply so, and go on being so through every empire that would rather they weren't.**

**One correction the drawing owes, because this is the kind of page that owes them.** A turtle carrying elephants carrying the world is *not* Hindu cosmology, though it is endlessly repeated as such. The stack appears in John Locke's *Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1690) as a story about “an Indian,” and Bertrand Russell repeats it in 1927; scholarship on the epics and major Puranas finds **no world-upholding elephant in them at all**, and Kurma is a pivot for churning rather than a world-bearer — which [No. 35](https://starstuff.earth/everywhere-that-turtles-go-zine.html) already says. So what Pratchett is riffing on is a *European* joke about India, not anyone's scripture. This drawing is therefore a novelist's invention drawn as a novelist's invention, and it is deliberately not filed beside [the five turtles people made](https://starstuff.earth/turtle-field-guide.html). They are not the same turtle, and that is the connection.
