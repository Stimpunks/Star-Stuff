# What the Rest *Stands On*

A Star Stuff collection · eleven pieces

Every other page here argues about the world. These eleven argue about *us* — what we think belonging is, how we intend to be right, what happens when we aren't, and how the work gets made.

---

These are the pieces everything else leans on. When a zine says difference is variation rather than deficit, or that a quotation has to be traced to its source, it is standing on something written down here once so that the zines do not have to keep restating it.

That makes them load-bearing, which is a structural fact rather than a compliment. None of them is the important one.

The test for what counts as a foundation, and what each of these holds up, are below the cards.

## The pieces

What each one holds up is set out below the cards.

### [What We Are For](https://starstuff.earth/mission.html)

The Aims · Foundations · the mission

*An aim you cannot fail is not an aim.*

*The mission of starstuff.earth, in eleven aims — each with the page where it is already being kept.*

Then how a collection of printable zines serves a foundation whose first business is mutual aid.

**Details**

The site had a page for what it holds, what it owes, how it is made and how it connects — and none for what it is trying to do. Eleven aims in four groups: belonging owed rather than earned and variation as the rule at every scale; then one settled fact followed honestly, every quotation traced to a primary, the checking published errors first, and science celebrated by being practised rather than by having its authority defended. Then what we publish — hopeful news better than the coverage of it, the scan including what we declined, and a harm never named without a graded ladder of responses. Then how the work is made: in the open, on Papert and Harel's constructionism, readable and printable and free — plus the experiment stated rather than smuggled, that generative AI is used here and is tested against whether it can serve accessibility, accuracy and transparency instead of costing them. The second half reads the Stimpunks mission sentence into its three verbs and puts this site under each, then marks the half a website cannot do: direct financial support, mutual aid grants and living wages are the foundation's first programmes, and no zine has ever paid anybody's rent. Ends with a table of falsifiers — the observation, available to any reader, that would show each aim had quietly been dropped. One diagram, one table, a “Not:” refusals set.

Founding Document · Foundations · the claim

*The argument at full volume, so the zines can be quiet.*

*On the cosmic origins of who we are, and why we belong — not despite difference, but as difference.*

The science of how we carry the universe in our bones. Six sections and eight declarations.

**Details**

Six sections and eight declarations. We are all made of exploded stars. Your skeleton is piezoelectric. The universe doesn't pathologize its own variation. What it means to say L★S. ### [Inclusion Safety: Owed, Not Earned](https://starstuff.earth/inclusion-safety-creed.html)

A Creed · Foundations · the ethic toward people

*Worth before worthiness. Harm is the only disqualifier.*

*Inclusion safety is not a reward for the worthy. It is the baseline we owe one another simply for being here.*

The first stage of psychological safety, made into a creed: worth before worthiness, with harm the only disqualifier.

**Details**

The creed states the whole of it before anything else: **your place among us is not a prize you win — it is yours because you are here.** Then *worth before worthiness*, and the qualification in full, which is two sentences long: be human, be harmless. No litmus test — not your diagnosis, your speech, your output, your history, or how easily others can make sense of you; there is no audition at this threshold. One disqualifier, and it is harm. Where **Timothy R. Clark** grounds inclusion safety in common humanity (*The 4 Stages of Psychological Safety*, 2020, Stage 1, quoted on the page: it is *“not earned but owed”*, and giving it is *“a moral imperative”*), Star Stuff goes one floor deeper — you were ★stuff all along, so belonging is constitutive rather than conferred. The hand-out version is the broadside *Owed, Not Earned*.

### [Six Principles and a Room](https://starstuff.earth/six-principles-and-a-room.html)

An Essay · Foundations · the ethic after the door

*In 6,825 words of a professional ethics code, the word *environment* appears once — in a sentence about the practitioner's inner life.*

*An ethic you can only point at a person is an ethic that will keep finding the fault in the person.*

A counsellor's code is addressed to two people. The distress is usually coming from a third thing, and the third thing cannot be struck off.

**Details**

Helen Edgar came out of an evening on ethics in her BACP training and asked whether there was a Star Stuff version. The answer is read off one real document. The **BACP Ethical Framework 2018** — binding until November 2026 — sets six core principles, and every one of them is a duty running between a practitioner and a client. Counted rather than asserted: in 6,825 words, *client* appears 173 times, *practitioner* 35, *environment* once (in a sentence about the practitioner's own moral energy), and *systemic*, *structural* and *institutional* not at all; the only *organisation* named is BACP's own charity registration. That is not an oversight — a code's sanction is registration, and you cannot strike off a building — but it means everything the code has no slot for gets recorded inside the person who came in. **Then the profession moved.** The 2026 revision puts *“Working ethically is working within relationships”* on its cover, drops the Latin, goes from six principles to twelve, and adds **anti-oppression** as a named principle — a real step, said so plainly. And the same count shows where it stopped: *environment* falls from one to zero, and both uses of *relational* sit inside the anti-oppression principle, locating oppression in power *between people*. Kadodia & Krueger (*Philosophical Psychology*, 2026) put the gap exactly: these injustices *“are not merely interpersonal – they are structural.”* The room does arrive in the new code — as a reason you could not do the ethical thing, with a duty to document why. Guattari's three ecologies name the missing band; the floor stays the Creed's, since a relational ethic widens the list of things that can do harm rather than dissolving responsibility for it; and the practice at the end is Helen's own *Ethodivergent Hearth Building*. A “Not:” refusals set of six, including the paragraph [No. 81](https://starstuff.earth/already-an-entity-zine.html) declined and this page declines too.

*The one that makes every other page falsifiable.*

*How a story gets smoother than the science, and what reading the primary source puts back.*

Six ways a fact goes wrong, and eighteen worked corrections — most of them ours, published, dated, and fixed in public.

**Details**

How a story gets smoother than the science, and what reading the primary source puts back. Six ways a fact goes wrong — the quote nobody said, the number that went stale, the story told backwards, the credit that drifted, the press release instead of the paper, and the finding that flatters you — with eighteen worked corrections, most of them errors we published and had to fix. ### [Who Is Holding the Candle](https://starstuff.earth/who-is-holding-the-candle.html)

An Essay · Foundations · the stance

*There is no view from nowhere. There is only an unstated view from somewhere.*

*Skepticism and wonder, learned at the same time and called uneasy neighbours.*

Two Sagans and one method — and the reason this site says *we*.

**Details**

Two Sagans and one method. Carl learned skepticism and wonder together — from parents who knew almost nothing about science, and from no teacher at all — and called them two uneasily cohabiting modes of thought. His son Dorion argues that the passive voice, the “objective” stance and the anonymity of the scientist betray the fact that every observation is made from a distinct place and time by a particular individual. Why the two modes are held at full strength instead of averaged, why the funding decides which questions get asked, and why an accessibility-first collection writes in the first person plural, names whoever caught each error, and publishes its own corrections. One diagram, a table of deletions, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [A Promise, Not a Finding](https://starstuff.earth/a-promise-not-a-finding.html)

An Essay · Foundations · what we may claim

*A finding can be overturned by the next paper. A promise can only be broken by the people who made it.*

*For forty-nine issues we have signed off *Love you down to your star stuff*.*

The subject is us, not the cosmos — and “down to” measures how far the love reaches, not why it is given.

**Details**

For forty-nine issues this project has signed off *Love you down to your star stuff*, and the grammar of that phrase has been doing the hardest work on the site. The subject is *us*, not the cosmos — and “down to” measures how far the love reaches, not why it is given. The star stuff is the extent of the claim, not its warrant. Which matters, because grinding the universe down and sieving it turns up no atom of justice in the powder: a belonging claim resting on “variation persists in nature” would be revoked by any paper finding that it doesn't, and the same move run backwards is how eugenics argued. Ours never rested there. Includes the sentence we *did* write once, in the colophon of Zine No. 1, and repaired in the same breath. Two rules follow. Every piece here is a lie-to-children in Jack Cohen and Ian Stewart's sense — honest only where it marks the rung it stops at, which is what “a rhyme, not a proof” has been doing unnamed — and the ladder has a moral limit as well as an epistemic one. And First Sight and Second Thoughts, from Pratchett's *Wintersmith*, names what the six gates and the public changelog implement, with the catch that only the second half can ever be automated. One diagram, two tables, a “Not:” refusals set. All three ideas are borrowed, and credited on the page's face.

### [Starlight](https://starstuff.earth/starlight.html)

An Essay · Foundations · the method, and who is running it

*The colour of the universe is an erratum. That is the whole argument for working in the open.*

*This repository is public — every page, every tool, every error.*

Making in the open, in the shine of the cosmic latte: a colour first published wrong, and corrected in public by the astronomers who got it wrong.

**Details**

Making in the open, in the shine of the cosmic latte. Average the light of 200,000 galaxies and you get a warm off-white someone named cosmic latte — and the first published answer was *pale turquoise*, wrong because the software assumed a reddish white point, corrected in public within weeks by the astronomers who got it wrong. The most-shared fact about the colour of the universe is an erratum, and that is the whole argument for an open repository. What is public here, in nouns: the pages, the ledger, the errors, the reasoning, the tools, the index — and the one thing deliberately kept private, your search query, which never leaves your browser. Then the machines in detail: six automated gates, what each one asks, and the fault that created it — 44 pages that printed blank, four links nested inside a link, three collisions every other gate passed, a live zine never listed in the sitemap, a page indexed at 12% that passed its own check. Plus the rules those failures produced: measure, don't count; wait on a condition, never a clock; a page nobody looked at reads UNREAD, never ok. And the division of labour underneath: machines hold ground, people take it — every gate here was written after a person found the fault. Ends with the first plain statement on this site of how generative AI is used, what it is never allowed to do, and why a smoothing machine is a strange thing to build a fact-checked site with. Two diagrams, four tables, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [Rainbow Serpents](https://starstuff.earth/rainbow-serpents.html)

An Essay · Foundations

*entropy increases in an *isolated* system — and the qualifier keeps falling off*

A criticism of our physics, arriving from outside our discipline, which turns out to be correct. Three of our own pieces walked up to this line and stopped.

**Details**

The second law says entropy tends to a maximum in an **isolated** system — one exchanging neither matter nor energy. A *closed* system exchanges energy but not matter and **its entropy can decrease**; conflating the two is a documented teaching error, and [No. 43](https://starstuff.earth/elimination-of-waste-zine.html) made it on this site. In an *open* system the law is not suspended but restated: internal entropy production stays non-negative while total entropy can fall, because the system exports it. **That is not an exception. It is how anything alive works**, paid for by low-entropy sunlight arriving and high-entropy infrared leaving. **Tyson Yunkaporta** (Apalech clan, western Cape York) argues that applying the second law to open interconnected systems is a *curse* in a precise sense he defines: not a lie but *“a true law or pattern applied where it doesn’t belong,”* powered by something in constant motion. The essay checks the physics half and finds it right — and begins by admitting that three Star Stuff pieces each reached an edge of this and stopped: No. 43 states the domain restriction and marks crossing it as its *leap*; [No. 56](https://starstuff.earth/noise-floor-zine.html) uses the law to show a floor is compulsory; [No. 69](https://starstuff.earth/your-shadow-is-the-centre-zine.html) shows the rainbow is anchored to the observer. Yunkaporta’s carved club is the argument as an object: an **ouroboros** etched round the head, which when rolled across clay prints *“an endless procession of snakes, head to tail”* — the closed figure and the open one, from one carving. Then the discipline the essay exists for: **three infinities held apart rather than collapsed.** It *declines to explain the Rainbow Serpent*, which is not one being and not ours, quoting it and stopping. And it features **Kaya Oldaker’s *Autism Infinity Dragon*** — public domain by the artist’s own release, and not a Rainbow Serpent, not descended from one. Its place here is geometric and checkable by looking: **the head does not take the tail.** The mouth is open and the plume passes beneath it — a lemniscate, which crosses itself and continues, where an ouroboros closes by eating. The one claim the essay makes about us is routed through the physics and our own symbol: **a deficit assessment is an isolated-system model of a person**, and people are not isolated. Refuses lineage between the three figures, refuses “ancient wisdom,” and refuses to un-refuse No. 43’s leap.

How this collection works

## What counts as a foundation

A simple test, and it is narrower than it sounds: **a foundation is a piece the other pieces presuppose.** When [a Kin zine](https://starstuff.earth/collection-kin.html) says a shark's cartilage is a secondary loss rather than a primitive state, it is leaning on a paradigm it does not stop to restate, an ethic about checking the claim, and a position on why difference is not deficit. Those commitments had to be written down somewhere, once, or every zine would have to relitigate them and none of them would have room left for its actual subject.

Which means Foundations is not *the important collection* and not *the hard one*. It is the collection whose contents are load-bearing for everything else — and load-bearing is a structural fact, not a compliment.

## Eleven, and what each one holds up

- **The aims.** [What We Are For](https://starstuff.earth/mission.html) — what this site is trying to do, in eleven aims, each with the page where it is already being kept and the failure that would show we had stopped.
- **The claim.** [The Manifesto](https://starstuff.earth/manifesto.html) — six sections and eight declarations, stating the whole argument at full volume so the zines don't have to.
- **The ethic toward people.** [Inclusion Safety: Owed, Not Earned](https://starstuff.earth/inclusion-safety-creed.html) — belonging is constitutive, not conferred, and harm is the only disqualifier.
- **The ethic after the door.** [Six Principles and a Room](https://starstuff.earth/six-principles-and-a-room.html) — a professional code names the environment once in 6,825 words, so when the harm is coming from the room there is nowhere to record it.
- **The ethic toward facts.** [Too Good to Check](https://starstuff.earth/too-good-to-check.html) — the six ways a fact goes wrong here, worked from our own errors.
- **The stance.** [Who Is Holding the Candle](https://starstuff.earth/who-is-holding-the-candle.html) — two Sagans on why skepticism and wonder are both required, and why every observation has a particular person standing behind it.
- **What we may claim.** [A Promise, Not a Finding](https://starstuff.earth/a-promise-not-a-finding.html) — the grammar of the signature phrase, and why a belonging claim that rests on a fact can be revoked by the next paper.
- **The method, and who is running it.** [Starlight](https://starstuff.earth/starlight.html) — what is public here and why, the six automated gates and the faults that created them, and the first plain statement of how generative AI is used on this site.
- **The paradigm.** [Neurodiversity Field Guide](https://starstuff.earth/neurodiversity-field-guide-zine.html) (No. 3) — the model everything else is written inside of, stated plainly enough to hand to someone new.
- **The self-portrait.** [The Garden and the Stars](https://starstuff.earth/the-garden-and-the-stars-zine.html) (No. 18) — what kind of thing this collection is trying to be: a garden rather than a stream.
- **Where we stop.** [Rainbow Serpents](https://starstuff.earth/rainbow-serpents.html) — the qualifier that keeps falling off the second law, and a Law that is not ours to explain.

**Two of the eleven keep zine numbers and still sit outside the register collections**, which looks like an inconsistency and is a decision. Nos. 3 and 18 were made in sequence with the rest, and the number records *when* — that is load-bearing for the dated [changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) and the number-keyed fact-check ledger, so it never changes. But neither piece is doing what a [Star Stuff](https://starstuff.earth/collection-star-stuff.html) or [Star Gazing](https://starstuff.earth/collection-star-gazing.html) zine does: they are not following a fact to an argument about belonging, they are stating the frame that lets the other zines do it. So they keep their numbers and live here. *The number says when; the collection says what*, and this is the clearest case of the two disagreeing.

A name collision we live with

**Zine No. 3 is called the *Neurodiversity Field Guide*, and it is not one of the [Field Guides](https://starstuff.earth/collection-field-guides.html).** The eighteen guides in that collection share a form — a catalogue of same-shaped entries with diagrams, reframe tables and collapsed field notes. No. 3 is an essay-shaped primer that happens to use "field guide" in the older, plainer sense of *something you can carry*.

Renaming it would falsify the changelog and the number-keyed ledger for the sake of tidiness, so the name stays and the collision is stated instead. It is the same call as keeping [Kin](https://starstuff.earth/collection-kin.html) rather than naming it "Ways of Being," which would have collided with Field Guide No. 5.

## The one that makes the rest checkable

If Foundations has a keystone it is [Too Good to Check](https://starstuff.earth/too-good-to-check.html), because it is the only piece here that can be used *against* the others. A manifesto cannot be falsified. A creed cannot be falsified. But a claim about piezoelectric bone or a quotation attributed to a named thinker absolutely can, and this site makes several hundred of them.

So the essay names the six failure modes, works eighteen real corrections through them, and points at the public record: the [changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) in [Notes & Rationale](https://starstuff.earth/collection-notes.html), where every error we have caught in our own work is published with what was wrong, what is right, and who caught it. **An ethic about facts that produced no visible corrections would be a decoration.**

Its companion, [Who Is Holding the Candle](https://starstuff.earth/who-is-holding-the-candle.html), argues the half underneath it. Checking is one of *two* modes and fails on its own; and the checker is always a particular person standing somewhere, so “verified” with no verifier named is the same deletion this site spends its time undoing. Read together, one says *go back to the primary source* and the other says *somebody has to go, and it matters who*.

Not

**Not required reading.** Foundations is what the site leans on, not what you have to read first. Most people should [start somewhere else](https://starstuff.earth/collection-start-here.html) and arrive here later, if at all, when they want to know why a zine was so confident about something.

**Not scripture.** These are positions we hold and argue for, and the manifesto in particular is written to be disagreed with in public. Nothing here is settled by having been declared.

**Not a code of conduct.** The creed is about what belonging *is*, not a policy document with an enforcement procedure. It names harm as the disqualifier and stops there, because a creed that pretended to be a process would be worse at both jobs.

**Not a claim to have got it right.** The presence of *Too Good to Check* in this collection is an admission, not a boast — it exists because we shipped a Martin Luther King Jr. line credited to Baldwin, a paraphrase dressed as a Sagan quote, and a forest-wide fungal network asserted as settled fact because it rhymed so well.
