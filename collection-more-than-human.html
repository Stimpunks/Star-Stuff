More Than Human

A Star Stuff collection · 7 pieces

Neurodiversity, de-anthropocentrized. Biodiversity contains ethodiversity contains neurodiversity.

What holds these together

Neurodiversity is usually argued at human scale. These pieces refuse that ceiling. Following Ombre Tarragnat's ethodiversity — the variety of ways of being and behaving across all beings with a nervous system — the nesting runs biodiversity ⊃ ethodiversity ⊃ neurodiversity. Not a rainforest but a chorus: ways of being, all of them whole.

So: the tick's three signals and the mantis shrimp's light, each a complete world rather than a partial version of ours. A council where soil, river, beaver and meadow all get a chair. Organisms that don't merely adapt to a fixed environment but build the environment that then shapes them. The octopus's mind distributed down its arms, the jellyfish's centreless net — no standard nervous system, not across species and not within one. And one population of sea otters that, put under pressure, fans out into individual specialists rather than converging on an average otter — with the ones who carry a stone keeping their teeth for it. And a freshwater polyp with no brain at all that sleeps anyway, and needs to, which is where any account of rest as something earned runs out of ledger.

A declared trilogy

The Council of All Beings, Every Creature's Own Sky and Minor Worlds are a loose trilogy, and Minor Worlds says so itself: how we treat other worlds, how we sense them, and then how we build them. They are kept together and in that order here.

Not

Not the wood-wide-web overreach. Mycorrhizal symbiosis — fungus and root trading minerals for sugar — is settled and ours to use. The forest-wide story is not: Karst, Jones & Hoeksema (Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2023) find the prevalence of common mycorrhizal networks and their benefit to seedlings insufficiently supported, and find no published evidence that elder trees preferentially feed their own offspring. We keep the kinship and name the contested part. It rhymes beautifully with mutual aid, which is exactly why it slipped past us in three pieces before we caught it.

Not speaking for other beings. Giving a creature a chair is not the same as claiming to know what it would say.

The pieces

In the order they were made. The number records when; the collection records what.

Umwelt as star stuff. Every creature is sealed in its own complete perceptual world — the tick's three signals, the bat's echo, the mantis shrimp's light, the dog's scent-time — and each world is whole, not a partial view of one real world underneath. There is no neutral, default way to sense. The animals do the arguing, so that when minds enter, the point lands as recognition: monotropism is an attention-world, Autistic perception a different and complete sky, not a broken copy of a standard one. And when two worlds meet — human and bat, one mind and another — the gap is mutual: the double empathy problem, arrived at without lecturing. Not one correct channel, but mutual translation. Carries the Umwelt thread from No. 14 inward, from von Uexküll through Tarragnat, Manning, Milton, and Despret. Eight spreads, six diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

8 spreads→
Details

Niche construction as star stuff, and the third of a loose trilogy — how we treat other worlds (No. 14), how we sense them (No. 15), and here, how we build them. Organisms don't just adapt to a fixed environment; they remake it — the beaver's dam, the spider's web — and pass the built world on as ecological inheritance. So the neurodiversity turn is plain: don't fix the mind, change the environment (positive niche construction — there is no “normal” brain in a vat). Stimming is worldmaking; minor worlds and niche communities are real, not lesser; and no niche is neutral — the “default” room was built for one kind of mind, so the fix is reconstruction, not accommodation. Grounded in Odling-Smee & Laland, Armstrong, Tarragnat, Manning, Kadodia & Krueger, and Haraway, and rooted in Stimpunks' own glossary. Eight spreads, six diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

Zine No. 27
Every Nervous System
Stimpunks Zines

on more-than-human neurodiversity, and the animality we reclaim

There is no standard nervous system — not across species, and not within one. Read neurodiversity literally and it never stopped at us.

8 spreads→
Details

More-than-human neurodiversity, read through star stuff. There is no standard nervous system — not across species, and not within one: the octopus's mind distributed down its arms, the jellyfish's centreless nerve net, the worm's fully mapped 302 neurons. Read neurodiversity literally and it never stopped at the human. Following Ombre Tarragnat's antispeciesist–posthumanist philosophy: there's “no valid reason to be anthropocentric when you talk about neurodiversity,” and ethodiversity — behaviour reclaimed as “a subjective response to a bodymind's situation” — is a fundamentally animal phenomenon. Ethonormativity punishes the ethodivergent (the horse who refuses the rider, the cat who resists petting — abandoned or euthanised). And the core move: neurodivergent people are pathologised by being animalised, so the liberation is to reclaim animality — not bestialisation (the speciesist, neuronormative “less-than-human” split, after Butler 2020) but (hum)animality, kin. Distance is not disconnection: shutting down can be how we keep relation. Every nervous system is the universe wired up to feel itself, no two ever the same. “I would rather be a divergent animal than a typical human.” Eight spreads, six diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

Zine No. 58
The Stone You Keep
Guest zine · after Helen Edgar

on otters, monotropism, and the measurable advantage of being specifically yourself

One otter population under pressure fans out into individual specialists, each measurably better at its own prey. Some carry a stone and some never do — and the ones with the stone keep their teeth.

10 spreads→
Details

Developed after Helen Edgar's essay on the otter cam that runs every Saturday at Solidarity Sesh, where nobody has to watch and that is the part that makes it work. Then past the essay to the papers. Tinker, Bentall & Estes (2008) followed one sea otter population closely enough to tell the individuals apart: where food was scarce it did not converge on one best way to be an otter but fanned out into distinct specialists — snails, clams, urchins, crabs — each with a matched suite of diving behaviours, and each measurably better at its own prey than a generalist. The averaged otter forages nowhere. Hall & Schaller (1964) counted the blows: a mussel took a mean of 35.5, and a range of 6 to 88, so there is no correct number of blows, only yours. Law et al. (2024), across 196 tagged otters, found tool use varies enormously between individuals and that non-users are not behind — they specialise on prey a stone would not help with — while the tool users showed less tooth damage. The accommodation is legible in the skeleton. Also: no blubber, the densest coat measured on any mammal and varying sixfold across one body, a resting metabolism three times what its size predicts from heat leaking out of its own muscle since birth — the calm surface is not the whole cost. They rest in rafts wrapped in kelp, so the holding is done by the forest rather than by anyone's effort; and the kelp is there because the otters are (Estes & Palmisano 1974). Held by what it sustains. Then the fur trade took ninety-nine per cent of them, because they were remarkable, not because they were deficient. Ten spreads, eight diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set of seven that declines cute, inspiring, superpower, rest-to-produce, and the smoothed retellings.

Zine No. 59
The Rest You Keep
With Helen Edgar · companion to No. 58

on Autistic rest, monotropism, burnout — and the ledger that was never there

Hydra has no brain and it sleeps — and deprive it of sleep and its cells stop dividing properly. Rest is not a reward released after enough work. It is older than the machinery of earning.

11 spreads→
Details

Built with Helen Edgar from her essay Reclaiming Rest: Autistic Burnout, Monotropism, and Resistance, whose inversion is the centre of the zine: rest that fights your attention is not rest, and recovery for monotropic people often means going deeper rather than switching off. Then past the essays to the papers. Kanaya et al. (2020) showed that Hydra vulgaris — a nerve net, no brain, nothing gathering it into a decision-maker — meets the behavioural criteria for sleep: it goes quiescent, it is harder to rouse, and after deprivation it rebounds. Deprive it further and the number of dividing cells drops, so rest is when the building happens rather than a pause in it. It also has no circadian clock: under constant conditions the daily rhythm vanishes and a roughly four-hour cycle remains, returning and returning. Great frigatebirds sleep a mean of 42 minutes a day on the wing against twelve hours ashore (Rattenborg et al. 2016), mostly one hemisphere at a time and while soaring rather than flapping — and a separate study found them aloft up to two months without landing (Weimerskirch et al. 2016), so rest is not the opposite of motion. Hibernating grizzlies and black bears go six months without weight-bearing movement and lose no bone at all, because formation and resorption fall together and stay balanced (McGee-Lawrence et al. 2009) — the bear does not out-work the problem, it turns remodelling down at both ends. Meanwhile the den lowers the energy bill: metabolism to about a quarter of basal with core temperature down only five or six degrees (Tøien et al. 2011). Rest as a property of an arrangement, not a feat of will. Closes on Tricia Hersey and the ledger: a debt that was issued can be refused. Eleven spreads, eight diagrams, and a “Not:” refusals set of seven that declines rest-to-produce, “just relax”, rest-as-project, meltdown-as-rest, and natural-therefore-allowed. Interoception gets its own spread, and this zine explicitly does not call meltdown or shutdown a form of rest.
