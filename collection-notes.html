We Left the Workings In

A Star Stuff collection · four working papers

The apparatus, published on purpose: what changed and when, every error we caught in our own work, how the pages are built for screen and for paper, and what the measuring tools still cannot see.

Why publish the apparatus at all

Most projects keep this material in a drawer. Ours is in the open for one reason, and it is a consistency argument rather than a virtue: a site that spends this much time telling you to check the primary source cannot then ask you to take its own work on trust.

Too Good to Check, over in Foundations, names six ways a fact goes wrong here. That essay is only worth anything if there is visible evidence of the checking actually happening — a dated record of what we got wrong, what is right, and who caught it. Otherwise it is a page about rigour, which is the cheapest kind of page there is.

So this collection is the receipts. It is also, frankly, the part most likely to be useful to someone building something of their own: the design system and the print rules are the accumulated results of getting it wrong first, and they are CC BY-SA like everything else.

Published, and not

There is a gradient here rather than a wall, and it is worth naming since this page is where a reader would wonder:

  • On the site. The four pages below. The changelog also answers at /changelog, because it is the one people link to.
  • In the repository, public but not a page. FACTCHECK.md — the full per-piece ledger, keyed by number, with every status and open item. Linked from the changelog for anyone who wants the long version.
  • In the repository, deliberately a working doc. DECISIONS.md says on its own face that it is not published — it's for us. It tracks choices made and choices still open, in a register that would be misleading as a public statement.

Nothing here is hidden; some of it is simply not a page. The line is about what has been written for a reader versus what is still shop talk, not about what we are willing to show.

Design
The Design System
Notes · how the screen works

Accessibility-first, printable, and credited.

Atkinson Hyperlegible, a five-accent palette on a violet-cast near-black, full-opacity body text at 18.84:1, and a starfield in pure CSS. Includes the lineage done honestly: what the palette borrowed from Solarized is the method — a named accent set at near-uniform lightness on a non-black ground — and none of the actual values, which are Tailwind's 400 ramp. Our contrast target is the opposite of Solarized's.

Design
Designing for Paper
Notes · how the paper works

Every number on the page, and why it is that number.

190 × 259 mm as the intersection of A4 and US Letter, so one sheet prints uncropped on either. Nine-millimetre margins rather than ten, because at ten the Letter box measures 259.4 mm against a 259 mm sheet and that sub-pixel overflow produced a silent third page. Ink on white in both media, because browsers omit background graphics by default. Borders instead of backgrounds; two spot inks darkened until they clear AA on white. And a section on what the tools cannot see.

Backing Material
The Difference-First Frame
Notes · the frame behind the frame

A resonant frame, not a proof — and it says so.

A summary of Helen Edgar's essay Why Difference Comes First, held here for anyone who asks why difference gets to be the starting point rather than a deviation from a norm: the cosmic microwave background needing tiny perturbations to make any structure at all, Deleuze on difference before identity, relational quantum mechanics, and ethodiversity. Kept as backing material rather than promoted to a zine, precisely because it is a resonance across four fields and not an argument any one of them makes.

Why this whole section is off the reading chain

Nearly every page in the collection is linked into a single prev/next reading order — seventy of them, running from Start Here through to the last broadside. All four pages above sit outside it, and so does this one — the only collection page that does.

That is deliberate and it follows from what these are. A changelog is something you consult when you want to know whether a claim moved; a design system is something you open when you are building. Threading them into a reading sequence would put a maintenance document between two zines and imply an order nobody wants. About and Cosmic Connections are off the chain for the same reason.

So they are reached the way reference material should be: from the front page, from the nav, from search, and from the pages that cite them. Being off the path is not the same as being buried.

Not

Not a disclaimer page. Nothing here is hedging the rest of the site. The changelog exists because the claims are meant to be checkable, which is the opposite of a caveat.

Not a completeness claim. The changelog records the errors we found. It cannot record the ones still sitting in a page nobody has re-read — and the ledger keeps an open-items list precisely because that set is never empty.

Not process theatre. These pages exist because they get used: the print rules came out of forty-four pages that printed blank, and the design system out of a contrast audit that found real failures. A document that recorded no failures would not be worth the file.

Not the interesting part. If you have limited attention today, spend it on a zine. This collection will still be here, and it is written for the person who has already read something and wants to know how it was made or whether it has changed.
