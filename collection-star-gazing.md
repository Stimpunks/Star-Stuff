# Star *Gazing*

A Star Stuff collection · 9 pieces

Awe-inspiring and not definite. Possibilities of thought, possibilities of connecting things and ideas.

---

The experimental shelf. These pieces are less certain on purpose: they follow a question rather than a finding, and they are content to end somewhere open.

Helen Edgar named this collection and most of it is hers. Each piece says whose it is.

Less certain is not less careful — guesses are labelled as guesses, and a contested reading is named on the page. The rest of the reasoning is below the cards.

## The pieces

In the order they were made. The number records *when*; the collection records *what*.

### [Why Difference Comes First](https://starstuff.earth/why-difference-comes-first-zine.html)

Zine No. 11 · Guest · Guest zine · Helen Edgar

*I've been thinking about the connections in-between — physics, philosophy, my own life as an Autistic person. This is me following that thread.*

A guest zine by Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms), in her own voice — one thread followed down three scales to the same shape: difference comes first.

**Details**

A guest zine by Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms), in her own voice, and the site does not translate it into house voice. Ten spreads following one thread down three scales — relational quantum mechanics, the cosmic microwave background, and Deleuze — arriving at the same shape each time: **difference comes first, and the baseline is the thing that got built.** It opens on *I'm not a fixed thing*, picking up her earlier writing on relational quantum mechanics, and ends where the flat line gives out. Filed in *Star Gazing* rather than *Star Stuff* because it is possibility-forward rather than one settled fact followed honestly — the physics is real, and it is being thought with rather than argued from. The backing notes are *The Difference-First Frame*, in *Notes & Rationale*.

### [No One Is the Leader](https://starstuff.earth/no-one-is-the-leader-zine.html)

Zine No. 22 · Stimpunks Zines

*on murmurations, emergence, and the whole that exceeds its parts — a flock has no captain, and neither does a self*

Each starling tracks only its six or seven nearest neighbours, and the whole emerges from that. After Ballerini & the STARFLAG project.

**Details**

Emergence and the murmuration — a flock has no captain, and neither does a self. Hundreds of thousands of starlings make vast rolling shapes with no leader and no plan: each bird tracks only its six or seven nearest neighbours (Ballerini & the STARFLAG project), and the whole emerges from that one local rule. Philip Anderson's “More Is Different” (1972) is the spine — the founding statement of anti-reductionism: at each level of scale new laws appear that the parts don't contain, so knowing the parts never rebuilds the whole. The early universe did the same, self-organising faint density ripples into the cosmic web with no architect. Two payoffs land it home: the whole person is realer than any diagnostic checklist of parts (you are not a list of deficits — you are the murmuration), and our best neurodivergent collectives — NeurodiVentures — are leaderless murmurations, not org charts (competency networks and catalysts rather than leaders; decisions where the knowledge lives). A “the whole exceeds the parts” zine. Held with care: leaderless is not structureless. Eight spreads, flock-and-web diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [A Mycelium and a Rhizome](https://starstuff.earth/mycelium-and-rhizome-zine.html)

Zine No. 26 · Stimpunks Zines

*how neurodivergent knowledge grows: no trunk, no center, many doors*

A tree has one trunk and a chain of command; you climb it or you're lost. A rhizome has no root to ask permission of, so you can enter anywhere.

**Details**

How neurodivergent knowledge and community grow, read through star stuff — no trunk, no center, many doors. A tree has one trunk and a chain of command; you climb it or you're lost. A rhizome (Deleuze & Guattari) has no root to ask permission of, so you can enter anywhere — Spiky Profiles → Monotropism → Deep Attention, or in from burnout, or a word that finally fit. No prerequisite, no gatekeeper. And a mycelium is the life in that shape: fungal threads carry nutrients and signals between trees that never touch (the “wood wide web,” Simard), thickening the paths that help and letting the rest fade; a brainless slime mold rebuilds the Tokyo rail network (Tero et al.). Zoom all the way out and the shape returns — the cosmic web of dark-matter filaments, the universe's largest structure, looks like a mycelium, like a neuron: not a pyramid, a web. We're already building it. Gray-Hammond and Munday name it neuro-anarchy; the Autistic Rhizome is “a network with no single point of origin,” members as equals held by a do-no-harm ethic, resilient because nothing depends on a center (with Helen Edgar's watering holes). Where the map meets soil is Cavendish Space. Decentralization isn't disorder — it's the oldest structure there is. Eight spreads, six diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [Wyrd & Weird](https://starstuff.earth/wyrd-and-weird-zine.html)

Zine No. 28 · Guest · Stimpunks Zines · Helen Edgar

*on the old word for fate, and the newer word we made of it*

Old English *wyrd* comes from *weorþan*, “to become,” and is twined with the root of “worth.” A guest zine by Helen Edgar.

**Details**

On the old word for fate, and the newer word we made of it — a guest zine by Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms), rehoused in the Star Stuff style. Long before “weird” meant strange, wyrd meant becoming: Old English wyrd comes from weorþan, “to become,” and is twined with the root of “worth.” Fate was never drawn as a straight line from birth to death but as cloth — the Norns' weaving at the well beneath Yggdrasill, the “web of man” — many threads crossing, none load-bearing alone. The Weird Sisters carried the word down through Holinshed to Shakespeare's Macbeth; over centuries “weird” drifted into an insult, and Autistic and disabled communities have run that drift backward, taking it up again as a name, not a verdict — which is etymologically honest, not just cheek. Held with care: wyrd is not a script, not doom, not a synonym for wrong, not something done to you alone. Under the root, weorþan meant “to become,” never “to be”: nobody is finished, and becoming was never the flaw — it was the design. You were never written; you are still being woven. Nine spreads, six diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set, a “threads already running” map of its kin in the collection, and Helen's own sources & notes.

### [Only in Relation](https://starstuff.earth/only-in-relation-zine.html)

Zine No. 29 · Guest · Stimpunks Zines · after Helen Edgar

*relational quantum physics, and what it means to be Autistic*

In Carlo Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics a particle has no standalone properties. Developed after Helen Edgar's essay “We Are Made of Relations.”

**Details**

Relational quantum physics, and what it means to be Autistic — a guest zine developed after Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms) from her essay “We Are Made of Relations.” In Carlo Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics a particle has no standalone properties; what it is becomes definite only in relation to another system (Rovelli, 1996). The count gives it away: two distinct coins make four outcomes, but two identical bosons make only three — what's fundamental isn't the standalone particle but the symmetry, the relation (Webster, 2026). Karen Barad calls the relation-before-parts intra-action; the human version, intradependence — made of each other, deeper than interdependence — Helen draws from Barad too. Read through Deleuze, “objects emerge out of more fundamental processes of differentiation”: difference first, then relation, and the tidy standalone object is the thing that got built. The payoff lands on our own work — the double empathy problem (Milton, 2012) lives in the relation, not one mind, and Helen widens it into DEEP; and in the ARLES ladder Relational sits near the base with Regulation inside it, because regulation is relational, so you change the field, not the person. Held with care: not quantum mysticism, not “everything is connected” as a vibe, not dissolution into sameness — the spiky profile survives the merge. A rhyme, not a proof. Nine spreads, seven diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [Always Migrating](https://starstuff.earth/always-migrating-zine.html)

Zine No. 30 · Guest · Stimpunks Zines · Helen Edgar

*migration, monotropism, and the galaxy you're already made of*

A guest zine by Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms), written after Brigit Anna McNeill's *The Living Page* workshop and rehoused here in the Star Stuff style.

**Details**

Migration, monotropism, and the galaxy you're already made of — a guest zine by Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms), written after Brigit Anna McNeill's *The Living Page* migration workshop and rehoused here in the Star Stuff style. Every migrating body is made of matter that already migrated once, so migration was never a metaphor borrowed from animals: it's one of the oldest facts about star stuff. A bird crosses an ocean on a compass sense we still can't fully explain — light striking cryptochrome in the eye makes a pair of electrons whose spins stay linked, and that linked pair is thought to be part of how it reads the Earth's faint field (Hore & Mouritsen; Mouritsen). Entanglement's lesson: once two things have been in relation, they stay in relation — separateness was always the less accurate story, which is Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics in a wingbeat. Zoom out and the pattern holds: the Sagittarius dwarf is still arriving in our halo, and the Milky Way–Andromeda collision everyone called settled is now closer to a coin-flip (Sawala et al., 2025) — a tug, not a destination. Then the turn inward: the bodily restlessness that builds before anything is decided is monotropism's gravity, attention gathered into a few bright points rather than spread as an even glow — and masking teaches us to quieten it until we lose our own north. Neuroqueering is the refusal; Pernille Fraser's “Can we trust the space you offer?” asks it from the other side. Widened by ethodiversity and the double empathy problem, and by Barad's intra-action — the liminal crossing isn't empty transit, it's where the shaping happens. Closes past the spiky profile: not a chart, a constellation of intensities. Eleven spreads, eight diagrams, a “Not:” refusals set, and a threads-already-running map of its kin.

### [Proportioned to the Groove](https://starstuff.earth/proportioned-to-the-groove-zine.html)

Zine No. 68 · Guest · Stimpunks Zines · Helen Edgar with Ryan Boren

*four lines of Dickinson, and why a narrow channel is a fit rather than a shortage*

Helen Edgar sets Emily Dickinson's J1765 against monotropism and the refrain — and it is the first zine here built to scroll rather than to page.

**Details**

“It is enough, the freight should be / Proportioned to the groove.” A *groove* in Dickinson's sense is the channel a load runs along, and the four lines say enough is a **relation** between a load and what carries it — not a quantity you could weigh alone. Set beside monotropism (Murray, Lesser & Lawson, 2005), that stops reading as consolation and starts reading as description. Deleuze and Guattari's refrain then supplies the mechanism in three movements, read at the primary rather than from summaries: a child sings in the dark and makes a centre in chaos; the circle gets drawn and *home does not preexist*, it has to be built; then the circle is opened a crack to join with cosmic forces — and they insist these are not three stages but three aspects of one thing, so nobody graduates out of the humming. Two passages are Ryan Boren's own, in his voice: the hallway outside his parents' door, and the freight of limerence. Carries its own corrections on the page — a cowbell line drafted as Deleuze *and Guattari* turns out to be Deleuze alone, speaking in *L'Abécédaire*, in a translation with only one author behind it; and the Sagan quotation is set in the wording Ryan confirmed against the footage, correcting six instances elsewhere on this site. Refuses retroactively diagnosing Dickinson, “narrow but deep” as a consolation prize, and the naturalistic fallacy. Eleven sections, a “Not:” refusals set.

### [One Word for the Opposite](https://starstuff.earth/one-word-for-the-opposite-zine.html)

Zine No. 80 · Stimpunks Zines

*On the vocabulary we built for what hurts, the single word we have for what doesn’t — and why a glimmer is *probably not a dopamine hit*, which is better news than it sounds.*

Trigger, flashback, hypervigilance, dysregulation, rupture, freeze, shutdown. The opposite got one word, in 2018. The asymmetry is the finding, and you can count it without settling anything.

**Details**

**Write down the words for a nervous system going badly** — trigger, flashback, hypervigilance, activation, dysregulation, rupture, freeze, fawn, shutdown, overwhelm — and then write down the words for a small moment in which one registers that things are all right. *There is one.* It is **glimmer**, and it was coined in 2018. That asymmetry needs no laboratory to check and no theory to accept: language grows where the pressure is, and the pressure was on harm, because nobody ever had to convince an institution that a moment of quiet was real in order to be believed. **What a word does is make a category countable** — findable, mentionable, recognisable twice as the same kind of event — which is a smaller and far more defensible claim than the ones usually made for such words. **The word is Deb Dana’s** (2018), offered as the counterpart to *triggers*, and it arrived inside polyvagal theory, which is influential and scientifically contested. So the zine answers the obvious question directly: *does a good word stop being good if the theory it arrived in is wrong?* No — words and theories are different objects with different failure modes, and **Helen Edgar says it plainer still**: glimmers need not relate to polyvagal at all, being simply the opposite of triggers, moments of joy and comfort, sensory or cognitive. Her monotropic reading is the definition the piece works from: a glimmer as an *alignment* of focus, sensory needs and emotion — which is why glimmer lists fail as advice, since the alignment is between a particular bodymind and a particular room and nobody else is standing in it. **The five nervous-system theories are named and deliberately not used**, with the observation the zine exists to make: Porges and Grossman & Taylor are not two parts of a synthesis but the two sides of an unresolved argument, Grossman & Taylor (2007) being the standing critique that RSA is too confounded by respiration to index vagal tone. **And the dopamine question gets an honest answer.** Berridge and Robinson separated *wanting* from *liking*: dopamine carries incentive salience — wanting — and conspicuously fails to raise the pleasure of a sweet taste. Liking runs on opioid and endocannabinoid signalling in hedonic hotspots about a cubic millimetre across, where mu-opioid activity can more than double liking reactions. A glimmer is not the pull toward something; it is the quality of a moment already arriving. **So it is probably not a dopamine hit — and that is better news, because the dopamine story quietly makes a glimmer into a reward that has to be chased, and the liking story does not.** Six refusals, including that noticing glimmers fixes nothing, that this is not a glimmer list, and that joy is not a duty or evidence of recovery. **Filed in Star Gazing, and the colophon says why:** three earlier attempts put this in Star Stuff, whose register demands one settled fact, and each either leaned on shaky ground or ended up with rigorous physics tied to the subject by a thread. The topic was never the problem. The collection was.

### [Strike “His”](https://starstuff.earth/strike-his-zine.html)

Zine No. 90 · Stimpunks Zines

*On the kinky, poly, gender-fluid, queer sex lives of Terran life — what a fish does to its own cell memory in ten days, what a fungus does with twenty-three thousand mating types, and why none of it is the argument we were reaching for.*

Ursula K. Le Guin invented a world with no fixed sex, then found the default she hadn’t noticed she was obeying — and printed the correction beside the error instead of over it. The animals got there first, and they do not authorise us either.

**Details**

**Remove the dominant male from a bluehead wrasse group and the largest female begins behaving as a male within hours.** Todd and colleagues (*Science Advances*, 2019) followed the whole cascade: a mature ovary remodelled into a functional testis in eight to ten days, sperm inside twenty-one, driven by changes to the chemical marks on the DNA — methylation at the *aromatase* gene among them — passing through an intermediate state whose epigenetic machinery resembles early developmental cells. *The cell’s memory of what it is gets rewritten.* **Anemonefish run it the other way and the zine uses them to correct its own author**: Fricke and Fricke (*Nature*, 1977) titled the finding *monogamy and sex change by aggressive dominance*, and what a ten-year-old reads as freedom is a rigid ladder in which the only way up is for somebody to die. **Two is not the number of sexes.** *Schizophyllum commune* carries roughly nine, thirty-two, nine and nine alleles across four mating-type loci — over twenty-three thousand combinations; *Tetrahymena* has seven; isogamous algae have no sperm and no egg, so the words have nothing to attach to. **And male pregnancy was paid for**: syngnathids lost the MHC class II pathway and the Toll-like receptors *TLR1*, *TLR2* and *TLR9* in order to tolerate the embryos in the pouch — a price, not a superpower. **Against all of it, Le Guin.** *The Left Hand of Darkness* (1969) gives the sentence the zine was almost named for — and the joke nobody quotes, which is that the Karhiders laugh at the pregnant king because he is *too old*. Then in 1987 she reprinted her 1976 essay entire, annotating it in brackets rather than revising it, *“hiding the evidence that one had to go there to get here”* — the collapsed “utter refusal” on pronouns, the regret that she had *quite unnecessarily locked the Gethenians into heterosexuality*, and the instruction this zine takes its title from. **The refusal is the spine.** Bagemihl’s 450 species were cited in the *Lawrence v. Texas* amici and it helped — and it is still the wrong argument, because the same sentence licenses traumatic insemination, sexual parasitism and infanticide, all named plainly rather than curated away. *Nature does not authorise us. It only fails to convict us.* Six refusals, one of them the author printing his own thirty-year misreading of bonobos against Mouginot and colleagues (2024), and one logging *autigender* as unsourced — because the people who coined it were not the sort of people whose records get kept, and that is a fact about the academy.

How this collection works

## What holds these together

This collection is named and described by Helen Edgar, who wanted somewhere for the more experimental thinking to live: *“i kind of like star gazing though — it is awe inspiring and not definite — it is possibilities of thought, possibilities of connecting things and ideas.”*

That is the register exactly. In [Star Stuff](https://starstuff.earth/collection-star-stuff.html) the settled fact carries the claim. Here the work is done by **philosophy of difference, relation and becoming** — Deleuze on identity produced out of a prior field of difference, Rovelli on properties that only become determinate in relation, the rhizome with no trunk and no chain of command, *wyrd* as the old word for becoming rather than fate.

## The hinge

*Why Difference Comes First* is where the collection turned. It is still cosmic — the cosmic microwave background, relational quantum mechanics — but it is the first piece to move from settled physics as a direct claim about matter into **philosophy of difference as the organizing frame**. Everything else here follows through that door.

Five of these nine are Helen's own, in her own voice or developed after her essays. The masthead is shared; authorship is not — each piece says whose it is.

Not

**Not a looser standard of sourcing.** Experimental register, same ledger. Speculation is labelled speculation, contested interpretations are named as contested — relational quantum mechanics is one interpretation among several, not consensus, and we say so on the page rather than in a footnote. Every quotation still traces to a primary source. See [the changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) for what we have got wrong and corrected.

**Not poetry standing in for an argument.** Being evocative is not a licence to be unfalsifiable about the physics we invoke.
