Start Here, or Don't

A Star Stuff collection · four doors, no queue

This is the head of the reading chain and it is not a prerequisite. Three of these are ways in and one is a way out — and any of the hundred-odd other pages is also a fine place to begin.

Why a start-here at all

There is a tension on this page and it is better named than hidden. Cosmic Connections argues that this project runs on links rather than folders, that its categories are outputs rather than inputs, and that nothing here arrived in a straight line. A section called Start Here is the most folder-shaped thing on the site.

It stays because the alternative is worse. A reader arriving cold at a wall of seventy-two zines and eighteen field guides is not being respected by the absence of a door; they are being asked to do the curation themselves, which is work we are better placed to do once than they are to redo every time. The fix is not to remove the door. It is to be honest that it is a door and not a queue.

So: nothing below is a prerequisite for anything else. Every piece on this site is built to stand alone, because that is what a person reading in a hard week can actually use. If a shark catalogue is what you can hold today, start there and let this page wait.

Four doors, and who each is for

  • You want to know what this is. What Star Stuff Is — the shortest honest version, in a few minutes.
  • You want the argument the whole site is named after. Love You Down To Your Star Stuff — one settled fact about where your atoms came from, followed until it says something about belonging.
  • You want to know how it is made. Cosmic Connections — the method, and the two lineages behind the phrase.
  • You want to write something of your own. We Are All Star Stuff — the open call. This is the door that goes outward.

Two of the four sit outside the reading chain on purpose. About and Cosmic Connections are reference pages rather than artifacts — you consult them, you don't read them in sequence — so the prev/next chain skips them and they are reached from the nav and from here. The other two are in the chain, which starts on this page and runs one hundred pages to Shorthand Evolution.

Details

Three sections. The shortest way in, and the page to hand someone who has not seen any of this before.

Details

Three sections, and the page names the method the whole site runs on. Three sites, one web: what travels between Stimpunks, More Realms and Star Stuff — essays becoming zines, glossary becoming argument, corrections going back out. Why links, and not folders: a web of linked notes produces concepts nobody sat down and wrote, which is why the collections here were observed after the fact rather than opened in advance. Accumulated, not authored — nothing here comes from nowhere. Cosmic Connections is Helen Edgar's phrase and it is the site's tagline; two lineages sit inside it and both stay credited wherever it is explained — James Burke's Connections (BBC, 1978) and Carl Sagan's The Cosmic Connection (1973). Two diagrams, a table of receipts, and a “Not:” refusals set.

Zine No. 4
We Are All Star Stuff
Start Here · the way out

The only door here that points away from us.

Voices from the community.

An open call for contributors — neurodivergent people and those who love them, writing toward what it means to be loved down to your star stuff.

Open call→
Details

The first three zines in this series made arguments. This one holds space for something different: the actual voices of the people the series is for. Nos. 1, 2 and 3 built the framework — the piezoelectric bones, the cosmology of care, the neurodiversity paradigm — and explained why you are made of star stuff and why that matters. We Are All Star Stuff asks what that means to you. We're collecting short pieces, a few sentences to a few paragraphs, from Autistic, ADHD, dyslexic, disabled and otherwise neurodivergent people, and from the family members, educators and community members who love them. In progress, and open.

If you only have five minutes

Read Love You Down To Your Star Stuff. Everything else on this site is that argument run through a different fact — a bone that turns pressure into charge, a turtle that breathes through the wrong end, a nervous system with no centre — and if the founding version does nothing for you, the variations probably won't either. That is worth finding out cheaply.

Not

Not a curriculum. There is no order you are failing to follow and no foundation you are missing. The numbers on the zines record when a piece was made, not what you have to read first — see any collection page for how membership actually works.

Not a difficulty ramp. These four are not the easy ones. Too Good to Check is in Foundations and is one of the most demanding things here; a field guide can be read in fragments on a bad day. Start with whatever your attention will actually hold.

Not an onboarding funnel. Nobody is being converted, counted, or moved through stages. There is no newsletter, no account, and no analytics on this site — the search runs entirely in your browser and the query never leaves it.

Not the only way in. Most people arrive from a link to one specific page and never see this one, which is fine and is how a web is supposed to work.
