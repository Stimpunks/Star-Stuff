Start Here, or Don't

A Star Stuff collection · four doors, no queue

This is the head of the reading chain and it is not a prerequisite. Three of these are ways in and one is a way out — and any of the seventy-odd other pages is also a fine place to begin.

Why a start-here at all

There is a tension on this page and it is better named than hidden. Cosmic Connections argues that this project runs on links rather than folders, that its categories are outputs rather than inputs, and that nothing here arrived in a straight line. A section called Start Here is the most folder-shaped thing on the site.

It stays because the alternative is worse. A reader arriving cold at a wall of forty-five zines and twelve field guides is not being respected by the absence of a door; they are being asked to do the curation themselves, which is work we are better placed to do once than they are to redo every time. The fix is not to remove the door. It is to be honest that it is a door and not a queue.

So: nothing below is a prerequisite for anything else. Every piece on this site is built to stand alone, because that is what a person reading in a hard week can actually use. If a shark catalogue is what you can hold today, start there and let this page wait.

Four doors, and who each is for

  • You want to know what this is. What Star Stuff Is — the shortest honest version, in a few minutes.
  • You want the argument the whole site is named after. Love You Down To Your Star Stuff — one settled fact about where your atoms came from, followed until it says something about belonging.
  • You want to know how it is made. Cosmic Connections — the method, and the two lineages behind the phrase.
  • You want to write something of your own. We Are All Star Stuff — the open call. This is the door that goes outward.

Two of the four sit outside the reading chain on purpose. About and Cosmic Connections are reference pages rather than artifacts — you consult them, you don't read them in sequence — so the prev/next chain skips them and they are reached from the nav and from here. The other two are in the chain, which starts on this page and runs seventy pages to Shorthand Evolution.

If you read one thing before deciding whether to read more.

A collaboration between the Stimpunks Foundation and Helen Edgar's More Realms: zines and field guides that read settled science as an argument about belonging, from the cosmic to the lived. Written in the first person plural, because we are writing from inside the community rather than about it. Printed free, shared freely.

The Page
Love You Down To Your Star Stuff
Start Here · the founding argument

The one the site is named after.

A cosmological argument stated as an act of love. Almost every atom heavier than helium was forged in stars and their deaths — the calcium in your bones, the iron in your blood — and that is not a metaphor. The page follows the fact honestly until it arrives at belonging, and gives the phrase its five registers: LYDTYSS, LYSS, LUSS, L★S, ★stuff.

Cosmic Connections
How It All Connects
Start Here · the method

Helen Edgar's phrase, and the house method it names.

How Stimpunks, More Realms and Star Stuff work as one interdisciplinary, iterative web — emergence over hierarchy, links over folders, and nothing coming from nowhere. Two lineages sit inside the phrase and both stay credited wherever it is explained: James Burke's Connections (BBC, 1978) and Carl Sagan's The Cosmic Connection (1973). This is also the page that explains why the collections were observed rather than planned.

Zine No. 4
We Are All Star Stuff
Start Here · the way out

The only door here that points away from us.

Voices from the community, and an open call for contributors — neurodivergent people and those who love us, writing toward what it means to be loved down to your star stuff. It keeps its number because numbers here record when, and it sits in Start Here rather than in a register collection because what it is asking for is not a reading but a reply.

Open call→

If you only have five minutes

Read Love You Down To Your Star Stuff. Everything else on this site is that argument run through a different fact — a bone that turns pressure into charge, a turtle that breathes through the wrong end, a nervous system with no centre — and if the founding version does nothing for you, the variations probably won't either. That is worth finding out cheaply.

Not

Not a curriculum. There is no order you are failing to follow and no foundation you are missing. The numbers on the zines record when a piece was made, not what you have to read first — see any collection page for how membership actually works.

Not a difficulty ramp. These four are not the easy ones. Too Good to Check is in Foundations and is one of the most demanding things here; a field guide can be read in fragments on a bad day. Start with whatever your attention will actually hold.

Not an onboarding funnel. Nobody is being converted, counted, or moved through stages. There is no newsletter, no account, and no analytics on this site — the search runs entirely in your browser and the query never leaves it.

Not the only way in. Most people arrive from a link to one specific page and never see this one, which is fine and is how a web is supposed to work.
