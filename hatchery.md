# The *Hatchery*

Twelve cosmic eggs · a specimen sheet · an easter egg

Twelve eggs drawn for this site. One of them is the small door at the foot of every page; the other eleven are the ones we drew on the way, kept because each carries something we can check.

---

The **cosmic egg** — the universe as something that was closed and then opened — is one of the most widely repeated images people have. It is in the *Rigveda* as Hiraṇyagarbha, the golden womb; in the Ogdoad's egg at Hermopolis; in the *Kalevala*, where Ilmatar's eggs become the sky and the earth; in Pangu's shell, which takes eighteen thousand years to open; in Taʼaroa's, in the Society Islands; in the Dogon Amma; in Zoroastrian texts that use the egg as an analogy for the shape of the cosmos.

**None of those is drawn on this page, and that is deliberate.** These twelve are ours. We name the motif and leave the cosmologies to the people who hold them — the same line [Field Guide No. 10](https://starstuff.earth/turtle-field-guide.html) drew when it put five *turtles people made* in their own section with no reframe table, because running *sounds like / actually* over somebody's cosmology would be obscene. A borrowed shape is not a borrowed belief.

What every egg here has in common is the thing the whole site is about: **something that looks like a single smooth object turns out, up close, to be made of variation** — and the variation is not the flaw in it. Twelve drawings, twelve arguments, one of them load-bearing.

Where this form stops

**A drawing is not evidence.** Each egg below is a picture attached to a claim, and the picture proves nothing — the source line under each one is where the actual work is. Where a claim is contested or thin, the entry says so rather than letting a confident illustration carry it. That is the failure mode this collection was built against: [a thing that rhymes too well](https://starstuff.earth/too-good-to-check.html) gets believed on the strength of the rhyme.

**The first egg is the one with a live argument against it,** and it is the one we shipped anyway. See [The Spiky Shell](#spiky-shell), which explains what it had to change to be usable here.

## The one on the door

This is the mark at the bottom of [the front page](https://starstuff.earth/index.html), and it replaced an egg emoji on 14 August 2026.

### The Spiky Shell on every page

A jagged line with nothing behind it to be measured against

An egg cracked open and lifted ajar, with sky inside instead of yolk. It began as a straightforward idea — *make the crack a spiky profile* — and that idea is wrong here, which is worth saying out loud on the page rather than quietly fixing.

**We have already published the argument against it.** [No. 21, *Not a Line*](https://starstuff.earth/not-a-line-zine.html) grants that the spiky profile beat the single severity dial, then shows what it kept: it still plots peaks and troughs as distances from a flat expected baseline, frozen at one moment of assessment. *A better chart is still a chart.* Its spine runs line → spiky profile → **constellation**, and putting a celebratory spiky profile on the front page would have parked our door one step behind our own argument.

So the drawing keeps the jag and throws away the apparatus. **There is no baseline and no grid** — the two things that made the profile a measurement. A crack in a shell is jagged because that is how shells break, not because it is scoring anything, and there is no line running through it from which its peaks could be deviations. What is inside is a constellation: stars in no order, with no rank.

On the profile and its limits: [No. 21](https://starstuff.earth/not-a-line-zine.html), built on Patrick Dwyer, “The Autism Constellation” (*Autistic Scholar*, 2020) and Helen Edgar, “The Constellation of Intensities: Neuroqueering the Spiky Profile” (*Autistic Realms*, 2026). The constellation metaphor belongs to the Autistic community and predates both essays — see No. 21's colophon, which names Caroline Hearst, Larry Arnold, Damian Milton and the late Dinah Murray.

## And eleven more

Four of these were the original proposals; seven were drawn afterwards to fill the sheet. None of them is on the door, and none of them is a lesser piece of work for it — an easter egg is a door that moved, not a standard that dropped.

### The Yolk Is a Star

L★S, held whole

Unbroken. The star sits where the yolk goes, and five rays reach out through the shell — one in each of the house accents, one for each of the five collections that sort by register.

This is the most brand-like of the twelve and the least argumentative, which is why it is not the door. It says *a star is inside this*, and the whole site says that already. It survives shrinking better than anything else here, so if the mark ever needs to work at favicon size, this is the one that would.

The accents are Tailwind CSS v3's 400 ramp, verified against v3.4.17 — see [the design system](https://starstuff.earth/design.html). The five registers are Star Stuff, Star Gazing, More Than Human, Kin, and Stars We Grew Up On.

### The Ossicle Egg

No two plates alike, and that is the shell

Twelve interlocking plates, no two the same size, shape or hue. The picture is borrowed from an animal already in the collection: the **leatherback** has no bony carapace at all — just hundreds of small interlocking ossicles set in oily skin — and it is the deepest-diving reptile alive, recorded to **1,280 m**, holding its body up to **18 °C** above the water around it.

Every item on that list reads as a missing part, and together they are the animal's range. The shell holds *because* of the variation in it, not despite it.

[Field Guide No. 10](https://starstuff.earth/turtle-field-guide.html), the leatherback entry, where the primary citations sit. *There is no standard turtle.*

### The Monotropic Egg

Attention as a tunnel, not a spotlight

Contours nested down to a bright core. **Monotropism** describes attention pulled strongly into a few interests at a time, with a great deal of processing going into whatever is in the tunnel and correspondingly little to what is outside it — which reframes a long list of “deficits” as the predictable shape of that distribution rather than separate faults.

Drawn this way the shell is not a barrier. It is the outermost ring of one interest.

Murray, Lesser & Lawson, “Attention, monotropism and the diagnostic criteria for autism” (*Autism*, 2005). Further reading at [stimpunks.org/monotropism](https://stimpunks.org/monotropism/).

### The Arrau Egg

The silence was ours

The giant South American river turtle, *Podocnemis expansa*, was filed for a very long time as a silent animal — until somebody brought a hydrophone. The recordings hold **2,122 sounds sorted into eleven types**. Hatchlings vocalise **from inside the egg**, then in the open nest, then in the river; adult females vocalise while basking and nesting, and in the river the females were recorded coming and answering. It is the first evidence of post-hatching parental care in any turtle.

The animal was never silent. **The silence was in the equipment, and in the assumption that made nobody go looking.** That is the entire reason this egg is drawn with the call going out *and* an answer coming back.

[Field Guide No. 10](https://starstuff.earth/turtle-field-guide.html), the Arrau entry, where the primary citations sit.

### The Collagen Egg

Press it and it answers in current

The founding fact of this site, drawn as a shell: **bone is piezoelectric**. Load it and it produces charge. Fukada and Yasuda measured it in 1957, and the skeleton you are sitting on is doing it right now.

**And the part that is easy to get wrong, which we got wrong ourselves:** the effect is *collagen's*, not the mineral's. Collagen fibres are arranged without a centre of symmetry and generate charge as they shear past one another — which is why the drawing is fibres, slanted, sliding. **Hydroxyapatite is centrosymmetric**, and that rules out classical piezoelectricity in the mineral. Our own [Manifesto](https://starstuff.earth/manifesto.html) credited the mineral, twice, until a fact-check caught it.

Fukada & Yasuda, “On the piezoelectric effect of bone” (*Journal of the Physical Society of Japan*, 1957); later work by Bassett and colleagues. The correction is logged in [the changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) and in `FACTCHECK.md`.

### The Egg Inside the Egg

It arrived; it did not grow here

A complete egg inside another egg, with a dotted line showing where it came in from. This is **endosymbiosis**: the mitochondria in your cells descend from free-living bacteria that took up residence rather than organs your lineage grew.

You are not one thing that developed. **You are a merger that held** — which makes “self-contained individual” a claim your own cell biology declines to support.

Lynn Margulis, publishing as Lynn Sagan, “On the Origin of Mitosing Cells” (*Journal of Theoretical Biology*, 1967), reviving earlier proposals by Mereschkowsky and Wallin; phylogenomics has since confirmed the core. See [You Were Never One Thing](https://starstuff.earth/you-were-never-one-thing-zine.html).

### The Supercooled Egg

Not how tough it is — what touches it

Painted turtle hatchlings spend their first winter in a shallow nest that goes **below −10 °C**, and they survive it by **supercooling**: staying liquid below the freezing point, because nothing has started the crystal.

Which means what decides whether one lives is **not how tough it is but what touches it**. A single ice nucleus at the shell — one contact — and the whole thing goes at once. It is the least metaphorical access argument in the collection: the outcome is set by the surroundings, and calling the difference resilience would be a straightforward misreading of the physics.

[Field Guide No. 10](https://starstuff.earth/turtle-field-guide.html), the painted turtle entry, where the primary citations sit. The same mechanism — supercooling, and critical slowing down alongside it — is turned on human thresholds in [The Same Water](https://starstuff.earth/the-same-water-broadside.html).

### The Onion Egg

The shell that actually made us

Late in the life of a massive star, fusion runs in nested shells around a dense core — lighter elements burning in the outer layers, heavier ones further in, iron accumulating at the centre where fusion stops paying. It is an onion, and it is also an egg: a closed thing that eventually opens, violently, and scatters what it made.

**The honest caveat, which this site has had to issue before:** “every element heavier than hydrogen and helium was made in stars” is *not* true as usually told. **Lithium, beryllium and boron are the exception** — stars largely destroy them rather than build them, and they come mostly from cosmic-ray spallation. Six of our own pages were making the tidy version, three of them already marked verified.

The correction, and every page it touched, is logged in [the changelog](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html). The smoothed retelling that files off the exception is exactly the failure [How We Got Here](https://starstuff.earth/collection-how-we-got-here.html) exists to mark.

### The Breathing Shell

A boundary that works only because it leaks

An eggshell looks like a sealed container and is nothing of the sort. It is drilled through with thousands of microscopic, trumpet-shaped **pores**, and those pores are **the only channels** by which anything moves between the embryo and the world: oxygen in, carbon dioxide and water vapour out.

Seal it properly and you do not get a safer egg. You get a suffocated embryo inside a perfectly intact shell.

**Protection and permeability are not opposites here — they are the same piece of engineering**, and the shell is tuned so precisely that its conductance has to match the embryo’s demand over the whole of incubation. That is the argument this site keeps making about the rooms we put people in. The ones that work are not the ones sealed hardest.

S. G. Tullett, “Pores and gas exchange of avian eggs: a review” (*Journal of Experimental Zoology* Supplement, 1987). **Figures approximate and stated as a scaling relationship, not a measured count:** pore number rises with egg mass from roughly 300 in a 1 g egg to about 30,000 in a 500 g egg, which puts a hen’s egg in the thousands. We have not seen a chicken-specific count we could check, so we do not give one.

### The Double Seam

The gap is between them, not inside either

Two half-eggs, facing. Both are drawn at the same weight, in the same hand, and their jagged edges **do not interlock** — there is a gap, and neither edge is the broken one.

This is the **double empathy problem**: the misunderstanding between Autistic and non-Autistic people runs *both ways*, as a mismatch between two differently-built minds rather than a deficit inside one of them. Look for the fault in either half and you will not find it, because it is not in a half.

Damian Milton, “On the ontological status of autism: the ‘double empathy problem’” (*Disability & Society*, 2012). Worked through in [Only in Relation](https://starstuff.earth/only-in-relation-zine.html).

### The Egg Not Yet Laid

Left open on purpose

A dotted outline and one faint star. This is the twelfth so that the sheet does not close: the pieces not made yet, the corrections not sent yet, the eggs somebody else draws.

It has a precedent here. The [Print collection](https://starstuff.earth/collection-print.html) contains a sheet that is deliberately blank, and it earns its place the same way — **the empty one is doing work the full ones cannot**. If you want to fill this in, [the call is open](https://starstuff.earth/we-are-all-star-stuff.html).

Sources & honest limits

**The world-egg list is named, not retold.** The traditions in the opening paragraph are named to credit a motif we are borrowing the *shape* of, and each is stated at the level of “this exists here” rather than summarised, retold or blended. We have not drawn any of them, and none of the twelve below is a version of anybody's cosmology. Where the popular framing of these motifs runs to *every culture had the same idea*, that is a smoothing we decline to make: the resemblance is real and the explanation for it is not settled.

**Every scientific claim here is one this site has already checked**, and each entry links to the piece that holds the primary citation rather than restating one from memory. Three are worth repeating because they are the errors we personally made: bone's piezoelectricity is *collagen's*, not hydroxyapatite's; lithium, beryllium and boron break the “made in stars” line; and Lynn Margulis published the 1967 endosymbiosis paper as Lynn Sagan, reviving Mereschkowsky and Wallin rather than starting from nothing.

**The first egg argues with us, and we left the argument in.** [No. 21](https://starstuff.earth/not-a-line-zine.html) is a case against the spiky profile, and the door mark started life as a celebration of exactly that. Rather than drop the drawing or quietly stop mentioning the profile, the entry states the collision and what changed because of it. A house style that only ever agreed with itself would be worth less than one that shows the seam.

**Why this page is an easter egg.** It is a specimen sheet for one small mark — genuinely useful if you are working on the site or want to take the drawings, and not something the front page owes a section to. It is unnumbered on purpose: numbers here are chronological and load-bearing, and a sheet of drawings for existing arguments does not need a date stamped into the sequence. *Same sourcing, same gates, different door.*
