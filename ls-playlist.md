# L★S Playlist

Twenty-six songs in conversation with [Love You Down To Your Star Stuff](https://starstuff.earth/love-you-down-to-your-star-stuff.html) — and the awkward fact underneath the whole rack: the sentence everybody credits to Carl Sagan was being sung to audiences three years before he published it.

Playlist & essay · Sound

One

## The order it actually happened in

This rack is not a soundtrack we assembled for a phrase we own. Put the four dates in a line and the phrase turns out to have arrived from the other direction.

**1957.** Margaret Burbidge, Geoffrey Burbidge, William Fowler and Fred Hoyle publish “Synthesis of the Elements in Stars” in *Reviews of Modern Physics* — the paper universally known by their initials, **B²FH**. It is the account of how the elements heavier than helium are built inside stars, and it is the settled science under every page on this site. Nobody set it to music.

**1969.** Joni Mitchell writes “Woodstock” without going to Woodstock. She had a television booking that Monday and, by her own account from the stage that December, got stranded at a New York airport and watched the coverage from a hotel room instead. She was performing the song live by **12 December 1969**. It opens on a traveller meeting a stranger on the road and ends on a congregation: *we are stardust, we are golden, and we’ve got to get ourselves back to the garden.*

**April 1970.** Her own recording arrives on *Ladies of the Canyon*. She had already given it away — “I wrote a song for that group to sing,” she told that December audience. “Actually I wrote it for myself to sing.” Both versions were in the world that spring.

**1973.** Carl Sagan publishes *The Cosmic Connection: An Extraterrestrial Perspective*, and on pages 189–190 writes the sentence this entire collection is built on: **we are made of star-stuff.** *Cosmos* follows in 1980, and chapter nine — “The Lives of the Stars” — is where most people actually met it.

*Drawn to scale: twelve years from the paper to the song, three from the record to the book, seven more to the television series that made it common property. The popular version of an idea does not arrive in the order that makes the neatest story.*

Two

## They are not the same sentence

It would be easy, and wrong, to say Mitchell said it first. She said something else.

Sagan’s line is a **claim about matter**. It can be checked, and it has been: the calcium, the carbon, the iron. Mitchell’s is not a claim about matter at all. *We are stardust* is the premise she opens with, not the thing she is arguing — the sentence she is actually building is an instruction about where to go next, and it lands on a garden rather than a supernova.

That difference is the reason this rack sits where it does. [LYDTYSS](https://starstuff.earth/love-you-down-to-your-star-stuff.html) is closer to Mitchell’s grammar than to Sagan’s. The star stuff is not the *reason* anybody is owed anything; it is how far the loving **reaches**. [Down to is not because of](https://starstuff.earth/a-promise-not-a-finding.html), and a sentence that makes the matter into the warrant has quietly changed the subject from us to the universe.

What we could not establish

Whether Mitchell got the line from the physics. It is the obvious question and it has an obvious flattering answer, which is exactly why we went looking. **Her own archive records no statement from her about where *we are stardust* came from** — not in the concert introductions, not in the song’s own entry. So we are not going to tell you she read B²FH, or that she didn’t. The dates are checkable; the influence is not, and a chain built across that gap would be a good story resting on nothing.

Three

## She was right twice, about two different catastrophes

Read the line literally — which is not how it was meant, and is where it gets interesting.

*We are stardust, we are golden.* Those are not the same forge. The stardust half is ordinary stellar nucleosynthesis: carbon assembled in red giants, oxygen and calcium in massive stars, iron at the end of the line. Stars, doing what stars do.

**Gold is not made that way.** An atom that heavy needs the r-process — a flood of free neutrons so intense it only happens in the most extreme places there are. Since the neutron-star merger observed in 2017, the best evidence points to **colliding stellar corpses** as the main forge. That is current best evidence rather than a closed question, and we say so here the same way [our own field guide](https://starstuff.earth/elements-field-guide.html) does.

So a lyric written about a music festival names two entirely different kinds of catastrophe in four words, and is right about both. Not because she knew. Because the words were true.

We got this wrong ourselves

The distinction between the two forges is not pedantry, and we know because we blurred it. [The LYDTYSS zine](https://starstuff.earth/lydtyss-zine.html) once said the elements were “forged in a *stellar interior*” — which is wrong for exactly the r-process elements its own companion field guide credits to neutron-star mergers. It is corrected, and the correction is [published](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html). A rack that makes a point of somebody else’s line being more accurate than they needed it to be should say where its own was less.

Four

## Then we aimed one of them at a star

On 4 February 2008, at four in the afternoon Pacific time, NASA stopped using its deep-space antennas to listen and used one to send.

The transmission was The Beatles’ **“Across the Universe,”** which is on this rack. It went out over the Deep Space Network from the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, aimed at **Polaris** — 431 light years away, by the figure NASA used at the time. It marked the fortieth anniversary of the day the song was recorded and the fiftieth of NASA itself.

“Amazing! Well done, NASA! Send my love to the aliens. All the best, Paul.”

Paul McCartney, in NASA’s own announcement of the transmission

Yoko Ono, in the same release, called it *“the beginning of the new age in which we will communicate with billions of planets across the universe.”* That is a larger claim than the event supports, and it is hers rather than ours — but the plain version is strange enough. A song is pressure in air. It was turned into radio and pointed at a star whose light left before the song existed, and it will not arrive until the **twenty-fifth century**. Nobody who made it, sent it, or is reading this will be there when it lands.

That is the whole of LYDTYSS in one gesture, and it is why this section is the one the rack ends its argument on. **The loving is aimed further than the lover can follow.** You do not send something four hundred years out because you expect a reply. You send it because the reaching is the point.

Five

## What the rack is, then

Twenty-six songs, four rooms, and a correction to the order we tend to tell it in.

It opens on Bowie because the whole career is the argument, then moves through cosmic origin, then tenderness, then the songs that take the word back. What it is *not* is a set of illustrations for a phrase we arrived at first. **We are the last people to this idea, not the first.** The physics was there in 1957, the singing started in 1969, the popular science caught up in 1973, and the phrase we built a collection out of came along half a century after the paper.

Which is the test this whole shelf is supposed to pass: take the audio away and the point goes with it. There is no version of this page that works as prose alone, because the argument is *when the singing happened*.

**Not:**

- **Not a claim that Joni Mitchell read the physics.** We looked, in her own archive, and found nothing either way. The dates are the finding; the influence is not.
- **Not a debunking of Sagan.** He never claimed to have discovered it. He was the great populariser and said so, and being the one who made an idea common property is a real thing to have done.
- **Not “the artists knew all along.”** The mystic-ahead-of-the-scientists story flatters everyone and checks nothing. A true line is not evidence of hidden knowledge; sometimes the words are just true.
- **Not the songs as decoration.** If you read a note here and do not press play, we have wasted both our time.
- **Not star stuff as the reason you are owed a place.** The matter is the extent of the claim, never its warrant.
