What We Are For

The mission of starstuff.earth, in eleven aims — each with the page where it is already being kept, and the failure that would show we had stopped. Then how a collection of printable zines serves a foundation whose first business is mutual aid, including the half of that a zine cannot do.

This site has a page for what it holds, a page for what it owes, a page for how it is made and a page for how it connects. The Manifesto is the argument. The Creed is the doorway. Starlight is the method and the machinery. Cosmic Connections is the traffic between three sites. What it did not have was a page saying, plainly and in one place, what we are trying to do.

That absence is not neutral. A project without a stated aim is a project whose aims can drift without anybody being able to point at the drift — which is the same argument this site makes about a number nobody can re-derive, or an ethic with no floor in it. So here is the whole thing first, and then the parts.

Star Stuff takes settled science, checks it in public, and gives it away free and printable — so that difference reads as the ordinary condition of everything there is, and belonging stops being something anyone has to earn.

Eleven aims follow, in four groups. None of them is a hope. Each one names a page where it is already being practised, because a values list you cannot check against the work is the exact thing this page is trying not to be — and the last section names, for each, the failure that would show we had quietly stopped.

What we are claiming

  • 1 · Belonging is owed, not earned. Inclusion safety is a floor, not a prize, and there is exactly one disqualifier at the door — harm, never difference. The sentence we say it in is Love You Down To Your Star Stuff: down to measures how far the loving reaches, not why it is given. The Creed · LYDTYSS
  • 2 · Variation is the rule, at every scale we can look at. Biodiversity, ethodiversity, neurodiversity and one shared planet, argued as the same fact seen through different instruments — and argued from inside, in the first person plural, because we are the people this is about. More Than Human · Kin · No. 8

How we intend to be right

  • 3 · Start from one settled fact and follow it honestly. The house method is a single checkable finding carried until the claim about belonging is already inside it — and where the reading is a resonance rather than a derivation, the page says so. We are noticing a rhyme, not collecting a proof. A Promise, Not a Finding
  • 4 · Trace every quotation and every coinage to a primary source. Aggregators, encyclopedias, our own glossary and any machine's summary are leads, not citations. A quotation reaches the page from the book, the paper, the essay or the recording, or it does not reach the page. Too Good to Check · the ledger
  • 5 · Publish the checking, and publish the errors first. Every audit gets a dated public entry naming what was wrong, what is right and who caught it. A living document that never shows a correction is a document making a claim about itself. The changelog
  • 6 · Celebrate science by practising it, not by defending its authority. Three things wear science's clothes and are its opposite — pseudo-science, scientism, and the smoothed retelling with every hedge filed off. Separating them is the work; none of it is an argument against evidence. How We Got Here

What we publish

  • 7 · Report hopeful science better than the coverage of it. Written close to the event and held to the slow work's standard: the primary read where it can be reached, the refusal named where it cannot, the qualifier the headline dropped put back. Faster to write is not looser. Glimmers · Glimmer Wire
  • 8 · Show the scan, including what we declined. A ledger records what we checked and a changelog records what we got wrong. Neither records the story we decided not to tell you, which is usually the most interesting thing about a week. The Held section
  • 9 · Name a harm accurately, then grade every response to it. Bad news with no exit is just harm redistributed to the reader, and this site is built by and for people for whom that redistribution has a cost. So every response carries a readiness grade and an individual-or-structural marker. Triggers

How we work

  • 10 · Work in the open, and learn by building in public. Seymour Papert and Idit Harel's constructionism is the reason rather than a decoration on it: knowledge gets built best when the learner is engaged in constructing something public. The pages, the tools, the reasoning, the ledger and the errata are one repository at one commit. Starlight
  • 11 · Make it readable, printable, and free. Accessibility is measured here, not asserted — contrast on screen and on paper, real headings, one landmark, keyboard reach, and a sheet you can print and hand to somebody. Everything is CC BY-SA 4.0 and made to be forked. Design · Print
And one experiment, stated rather than smuggled

Generative AI facilitates a great deal of the work here — drafting, the gates, the search index, source legwork, the ledger, the sweeps that touch every page at once. Star Stuff is, among other things, a test of whether that can be done in service to accessibility, accuracy and transparency rather than at their expense.

The honest summary is that a language model is a smoothing machine pointed at a project built to resist smoothing. It earns its place only under constraints a person maintains: it never decides what is worth making, it never stands as a citation, and it is never the last check on a quotation. The full statement, with what it is never allowed to do, and the time the mechanism failed here.

How this serves Stimpunks

Star Stuff is a collaboration between the Stimpunks Foundation and More Realms, and Stimpunks is a 501(c)(3) with a mission of its own, published on its own site. It is worth quoting rather than paraphrasing, because the paraphrase is where a mission usually goes soft.

“We exist for the direct support and mutual aid of neurodivergent and disabled people.”

“Stimpunks builds accessible infrastructure and public tools that support disabled and neurodivergent people to live with dignity, challenge dehumanizing systems, and design environments that honor difference as strength.”

Stimpunks Foundation · Mission

The second sentence has three verbs in it, and this site is a contribution to each one. It is also plainly not a contribution to the first sentence, and that distinction is the whole of this section.

Stimpunks describes what it publishes as “open learning pathways, a glossary, and a field guide anyone can use.” Star Stuff is what happens when that material is taken all the way through to an artifact: a thing with a cover, a diagram, a colophon and a print stylesheet, that someone can hold. If stimpunks.org is the garden, this is where it blooms — and a flower's whole purpose is to give its seeds away.

Two of Stimpunks' commitments show up here as constraints rather than as sentiment. “Everything we build is by and for our community — not handed down to it” is why this site writes we and not they, why Helen Edgar's pieces are hers under a shared masthead, and why the observer is named rather than dissolved into a passive voice. And mutual aid is not charity — which is why corrections here are treated as something readers give us rather than something we tolerate from them.

The limit, said plainly

Stimpunks' first programme is direct financial support to individuals. This site does not do that, and should not be counted as though it did. No zine here has paid anybody's rent, replaced a wheelchair, or covered a utility bill. If the argument on these pages is worth anything to you, the thing that converts it into material help is the foundation, not the artifact.

The honest claim for a website is narrower and still worth making: it is what a person can reach at two in the morning, for free, without an account, in a browser, when they have just been told again that the way they are is a deficit.

How you could tell if we stopped

An aim you cannot fail is not an aim. Each of these is a condition that could be observed from outside, by a reader with no access to our intentions — which is the only kind of accountability worth publishing.

the aim, and the observation that would falsify it
AimWhat would show we had stopped
Belonging is owedAny sentence here that makes belonging conditional on a diagnosis, an output, a level of function, or an ability to explain yourself.
Primary sourcesA quotation on this site whose citation trail ends at a quote site, an encyclopedia, one of our own pages, or a machine's summary.
Publish the checkingA stretch of changelog with no correction in it. We are not getting better that fast; a clean run means we stopped looking.
Better than the coverageA Glimmers entry resting on a press release alone, with no statement of what could not be read at source.
Bad news with an exitA Trigger that names a harm and stops, or a solutions list that puts an individual action and a structural one on the same footing.
Working in the openA count printed in prose that nobody can re-derive from the repository — or any of the eight gates left standing at a baseline above zero.
Readable and printableA page that comes off a printer blank, or a contrast failure shipped and left. Both have happened; both are measured now.
AI under constraintA page shipped whose sources no person read, or a citation that traces back only to something a model said.

Most of those are not hypothetical. Forty-four of forty-six pages once printed blank. A line by Martin Luther King Jr. shipped under James Baldwin's name. A forest-wide fungal network went into three pieces unchallenged because it rhymed with everything we already believed. A sentence out of this repository's own working notes was promoted to “our masthead” and argued against. Each of those is in the changelog under its own date, with the name of whoever caught it — which is what the aim actually looks like when it is being kept.

What this is not

Not:
  • Not a promise the universe makes. Nucleosynthesis does not entail that anyone is loved. Love You Down To Your Star Stuff is a promise made by people, and dressing it as a finding would be the naturalistic fallacy with a telescope attached. The argument in full.
  • Not a growth target. There is no newsletter, no account, no funnel and no analytics. Search runs entirely in your browser and the query never leaves it. We are open about our work; we are not curious about yours.
  • Not a substitute for material support. See above, and mean it. Publishing is not mutual aid, and a site that let the two blur would be doing the thing it criticises.
  • Not a claim to speak for every neurodivergent or disabled person. The first person plural here is a statement about where we are standing, not a mandate. Nobody elected us.
  • Not neutral, and not pretending to be. There is no view from nowhere — only an unstated view from somewhere. Declaring the position is what makes it possible to hold us to it.
  • Not an advertisement for AI. The machinery earns its place under constraints a person enforces. Take the constraints away and you get a fluent, confident, well-formatted version of exactly what this site exists to argue against.
  • Not finished, and not a founding document. This page was written after a hundred and fifty-odd pieces already existed, which means it is a description of what we turned out to be doing at least as much as a plan. If it stops matching the work, the page is what is wrong.

The shorter version

A knowledge garden is how star stuff comes to know itself, and it cannot do that behind a login. So: take something the universe has already settled, check it until you would be willing to be wrong about it in public, and hand it to somebody free, in a form they can print and pass on — on the understanding that the belonging was never in question and the only thing being supplied is the evidence.

That is the whole aim. Everything else on this page is a way of being caught if we stop.
