Side A of B

Six Ways
a Fact Goes Wrong

A story that fits gets less scrutiny, which is exactly when it needs more. Six shapes the failure takes — and the tell for each.

01
The quote nobody said

What circulates is a compression — true in spirit, absent from the text.

Tell More quotable than anything anyone says out loud.

Do Search the actual book.

02
The number that went stale

Measurements get superseded. The old value circulates for decades with nothing marking it expired.

Tell Repeated everywhere, dated nowhere.

Do Check the source you cited gives your number.

03
The story told backwards

The facts survive the retelling and the emphasis doesn't, so the meaning inverts while every sentence stays defensible.

Tell The paper's own hedge is missing.

Do Read what the authors said they don't know.

04
The credit that drifted

Attribution decays toward tidiness. A collective becomes a founder; a documenter becomes a coiner.

Tell One name where a community was.

Do Find the first printed instance, not the famous one.

05
The press release, not the paper

A layer whose job is to make the finding interesting. Its output isn't a lie — it's a livelier verb.

Tell The vivid verb is doing the work.

Do Open the abstract. See which word the authors chose.

06
The finding that flatters you

The other five are accidents. This one is an appetite, and it comes looking for you.

Tell You wanted it true before you read it.

Do Slow down here. Don't speed up.

Side B of B
The habits
Trace it to the primary.The book, the paper, the talk. Aggregators and summaries are leads, not citations.
Cite the source you actually read.If you haven't opened it, it doesn't go in the references.
Read your own siblings.Check what you've already claimed about the same thing.
When one source falls, re-check its relatives.Errors travel with their quotes.
Quote or paraphrase — never dress one as the other.Verbatim and in marks, or in your own words with the idea attributed.
Say what's still open.“Leading hypothesis, not a settled result” costs one sentence.
Log the correction in public.With a date, and the name of whoever caught it.
Measure rather than assume.A stated principle nobody checks is a wish.
Five we got wrong
The forest is wired together.Elders feeding shaded seedlings through the fungal network — in three of our pieces. Karst, Jones & Hoeksema (2023) find no published evidence for the elder-feeds-offspring claim. Mode 06.
“We are star stuff harvesting sunlight.”Not in the book — but he said it aloud: “We are star stuff. Harvesting starlight.” Circulation changed one word. Our first correction checked only the book and called it an invention, so the debunk was wrong too. Mode 01, twice.
Orion's Belt is scattered in depth.Hipparcos figures for stars Hipparcos measured badly. VLTI and Gaia put all three at one depth — siblings from one cloud. Mode 02.
Neurodiversity was coined by one author in 1998.Developed collectively by the online Autistic community; reached print via Harvey Blume, who said so (Botha et al., 2024). Mode 04.
Disgust is learned.Ours, and wrong as we stated it. Distaste is present at birth. Caught by Helen Edgar; the zine gained a spread. Mode 06.
Not:

Not “do your own research.” Tracing a claim moves toward the evidence and ends somewhere checkable. That phrase moves away from it and ends where nothing can be checked at all.

Not science literacy as a gate. Nobody owes anybody a reading list before they're allowed to speak about their own life.

Not the pleasure of the debunk. A Myth! headline is exactly as smooth and retellable as the thing it debunks.

Not contempt for the people who repeat the story. We repeated it. In three pieces.

The real thing was rarely the thing that needed improving.
