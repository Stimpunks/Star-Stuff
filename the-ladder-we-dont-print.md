# The Ladder We *Don't Print*

[ARLES](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/arles/) is the [Stimpunks Design Method](https://stimpunks.org/design/) — five layers of neurodivergent life in an order that has to hold. It is named here, and deliberately almost nowhere in the zines.

A spread-by-spread map: the pair closest to the ladder, three more that climb it without being built to, two that sit on a rung and say so — and the four pieces that already name the method out loud, which is a fact this page has to hold rather than tidy away.

---

What this is, and isn't

This is **a reading of our own work, and a reading can be argued with.** Every rung label below was applied after the fact, by us, to pieces that were mostly not built with the ladder in hand. Nothing on this page was measured. The six gates in this repository can check a colour, a tag tree, a page count, a sitemap entry and a text collision; not one of them can check whether a spread is really about attention.

The method was: read the spread eyebrows and headings of all **sixty-two numbered zines**, then read the candidates in full. That will have missed a piece that climbs quietly, and it may have over-read one that doesn't. If a mapping below looks wrong to you, it might be. **And the survey has a date on it:** sixty-two was the whole shelf on 23 August 2026, and the shelf has grown since — Nos. 63–72 have not been read against the ladder. That number is left as written rather than quietly raised, because raising it would claim a survey that did not happen.

Stimpunks crosswalks other people's frameworks against ARLES — [the ecosystemic model of distress](https://stimpunks.org/design/ecosystemic-model-of-distress/), and Jessica Dark's [System-Level Organisational Design Framework](https://stimpunks.org/design/slodf/). This page turns the same instrument on our own shelf, which is a much easier exercise and worth much less. *Finding your own method in your own artifacts is the least surprising result available.* What it is good for is legibility: it tells a reader why the zines keep arriving at the room instead of the person.

## The ladder, named once

ARLES names five layers through which neurodivergent life is structured, and the order in which environments have to be redesigned to support it. Each layer holds up the one above it.

A · Attention

How a mind focuses, processes, and forms meaning. [Monotropism](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/monotropism/) lives here. This is the floor.

R · Relational (incl. Regulation)

Communication, [co-regulation](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/co-regulation/), consent. Regulation is relational and environmental, not an individual skill.

L · Lived Experience

The sensory, emotional and cognitive realities of a day. What it actually feels like to be in the room.

E · Environment

The conditions that shape participation. A space either fits a [bodymind](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/bodymind/) or fights it.

S · Systems

The structures, norms and institutions that distribute opportunity and constraint.

Upward it is a design order · downward it is a diagnosis

Read **upward**, it is a sequence for design: begin with attention and nervous-system safety, build shared language and co-regulation, name what people actually experience, redesign the environment, and only then move to systems. Read **downward**, it is a sequence for diagnosis: a failing system points to the environment that produced it, the environment to the lived experience it ignored, and on down to the attention it never accounted for.

Skip the lower layers and systems reform collapses.

That sentence is the whole reason the order is load-bearing rather than decorative, and it is why most reform fails: it starts at the top, rewrites the policy, and then asks people to cope. ARLES and the design method it belongs to are Stimpunks' own, published in the open — [the glossary entry](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/arles/) is the short form and [the design method](https://stimpunks.org/design/) the long one.

## Why the word stays off the spreads

Three reasons, in the order they matter.

### One · A named framework asks for assent before the argument does

A zine here works by taking one settled, checkable fact and following it honestly until the belonging claim is already inside it. The Moon runs the tide because tidal force falls off with the cube of distance. A hammer and a feather land together in vacuum. Bone under load makes a charge. The reader is not asked to agree to anything; they are asked to look.

An acronym reverses that. It asks the reader to accept a five-part model of neurodivergent life *first*, and then the fact arrives as an illustration of the model — which is exactly the demotion the collection exists to refuse. **The fact stops doing the work the moment it becomes an example.**

### Two · The rungs already have plain words

Attention. The people near you. What the day actually feels like. The room. Who set the rules. Every rung has an ordinary-language form that costs a reader nothing, and the zines already use them. A piece that says *the storm is in the weather, not the water* has delivered the Environment rung to someone who will never need the label. A piece that says *when a feather falls slowly, look for the air* has delivered the downward read.

### Three · It is already the standing rule, written into a colophon

[No. 59, *The Rest You Keep*](https://starstuff.earth/the-rest-you-keep-zine.html), is the one zine built on the ladder deliberately, and its own colophon states the rule:

Internally this zine is built on the ARLES ladder — attention, relational, lived experience, environment, systems — which is deliberately not printed on the spreads; the argument should stand without the scaffold.

One precision, because the sentence is slightly generous to itself. What is withheld is the **acronym, the diagram and the order-claim**. The rung *words* are not withheld: five of them are printed on No. 59 as spread eyebrows, one per spread. That is the line, and it is the right line. A reader meets *Attention* as a heading over an argument about attention and loses nothing. They never meet a ladder they have to buy into.

## The one the ledger says was built on it: No. 59

[*The Rest You Keep*](https://starstuff.earth/the-rest-you-keep-zine.html) runs the full ladder in order, one rung per spread, with the acronym absent. It is the only piece whose own colophon and ledger row both record the ladder as an internal structure — *and it turns out not to be alone on the ladder, only alone in admitting it.* Its companion is the next section. Its five rung spreads are interleaved with three animal-fact spreads, and the interleaving is the interesting part.

2**The finding.** *Hydra* has no brain and sleeps anyway — and its cells stop dividing when you stop it.off-ladder · the fact

3**The tunnel, not the line.** No circadian clock to free-run; a ~4-hour cycle, returning and returning. Rest as a shape attention takes.Attention

4**On the wing.** Frigatebirds sleeping 42 minutes a day aloft. Rest is not the opposite of motion.off-ladder · the fact

5**Partial presence is real presence.** Camera off, lurking, reading and not answering — absence is a claim about output, not about being there.Relational

6**No ramp, just the edge.** Interoception arriving quiet or late, so the depletion never announces itself while it builds.Lived Experience

7**Six months, no load.** The bear's skeleton holding its bone through hibernation, against everything disuse should do to it.off-ladder · the fact

8**Rest is a property of an arrangement.** The den does a measurable job in the energy budget. Rest is something a setup affords, not something a body achieves.Environment

9**The debt that was never owed.** The earning frame — rest released after enough output — named and refused.Systems

10**Held honestly.** The refusals set.off-ladder · refusals

Spreads 1 and 11 are the cover and the closing question, as on every zine.

**The off-ladder spreads are not gaps. They are what each rung is paid for with.** Five consecutive rung spreads would be a slide deck: five assertions in a row, each asking to be believed on the strength of the one before it. Instead an animal arrives, does something checkable, and the rung it licenses follows immediately. The ladder is the spine; the facts are the ribs.

One thing this page cannot settle, and should say so: the spread-by-spread structure of No. 59 is **Helen Edgar's**, from her *Reclaiming Rest* and *Going Deeper*, and the ARLES reading is recorded in the finished colophon. Who placed which rung where is not recoverable from the record here, and the piece does not need it to be.

## Its companion, where the reading came first and the word never arrived

[**No. 58, *The Stone You Keep***](https://starstuff.earth/the-stone-you-keep-zine.html) is the same Saturday room as No. 59 — the [Solidarity Sesh](https://stimpunks.org/) otter cam, developed after Helen Edgar — following the otters where its companion follows rest. **Its proposal carried a rung-by-rung reading of the outline before the zine was built.** And the finished page names ARLES *nowhere*: not on a spread, not in the colophon, not in a `<meta>` tag, not even in its own row in the ledger. Zero occurrences in the file.

That makes it the best illustration on this page of the rule the page is arguing for, and better than No. 59: **the rungs went in, the label never got as far as the page, and a reader cannot tell.**

2**Nobody has to watch.** Cameras mostly off, some speaking, some in text, some just there — and the fact that nobody has to watch is what makes it work.Relational

3**There is no standard otter.** Under food limitation the population does not converge on one best way to be an otter. It fans out into specialists (Tinker, Bentall & Estes, 2008).Attention

4**The stone is not a metaphor.** A stone kept on the chest and reused from one food item to the next — and the blow counts run 6 to 88, so *the variance is the finding*.Attention

5**Some otters never use a stone.** Non-users are not the ones who haven't figured it out; they specialise on prey a stone wouldn't help with — and the accommodation shows up as *tooth health* (Law et al., 2024).Attention

6**What it costs to float.** The most restful image available to a human being is thermodynamically a furnace. The calm surface is not the whole cost.Lived Experience

7**Anchored, and together.** Rafts in the kelp canopy — and *the kelp* does the holding, not vigilance, not grip strength, not a sleeping animal working at staying put.Relational + Environment

8**The forest that holds you is the forest you hold.** Islands with otters had kelp forest; islands without had urchin barrens (Estes & Palmisano, 1974). Presence is load-bearing.Environment

9**What they came for was the coat.** A hundred and seventy years of fur trade, roughly 99% gone by 1911 — the trait that let the animal live in cold water is what made it a commodity. Plus the refusals.Systems

Spread 1 is the cover, spread 10 the closing questions. The proposal ran to ten spreads with the fur trade and the refusals separate; the built page merged them into spread 9, so the rung numbers transfer without renumbering — which is worth checking rather than assuming, since a mapping quoted from a proposal against a shipped page is exactly the kind of figure that goes stale quietly.

**It does not climb cleanly, and the way it fails to is a third pattern.** Every rung is present and the overall movement is upward, but *Lived Experience and Relational are transposed* — the cost of floating (6) arrives before the raft that holds you (7) — and spread 7 carries two rungs at once, because in an otter raft the relation *is* the environment: the kelp is what holds the animal, and the other otters are in the same kelp. So this one runs Relational, Attention×3, Lived Experience, Relational and Environment together, Environment, Systems. Set beside [No. 15](#map-no-15), which descends to the floor before climbing, and [No. 16](#not-a-ladder), which runs out of order because its claim is about the rung itself, that is the third distinct way a real zine declines to be a staircase.

The pair is the argument

Nos. 58 and 59 are companions by design — the same Saturday, the same room, the otters in one and the rest in the other — and they are the two pieces here closest to the ladder. **One prints five rung names as spread eyebrows. The other prints the word nowhere at all.**

Read them side by side and nothing about the strength of either argument tells you which is which. *That is the entire case for keeping the acronym off the spreads*, and it is an accident of how the two were made rather than a demonstration anyone set up — which is the only reason it is worth anything.

## Three that climb without being built to

These were not designed against the ladder. They arrive at it, which is the more interesting result — and in one case they arrive at it from the top.

### No. 17 · The Nearest Body — four rungs, and the floor missing

2**Tidal force.** The near body wins: tidal force falls off with the cube of distance, so nearness beats mass.off-ladder · the fact

3**Co-regulation is gravity.** A safe-enough nervous system nearby pulls yours into rhythm, at a distance, without touching.Relational

4**Spring & neap.** Aligned or crossed pulls; parallel presence as tidal locking.Relational

5**When there is no moon.** Meerkat mode: no steady pull, so the water heaves every direction at once. What the day feels like.Lived Experience

6**The storm is in the weather.** Water has no weather of its own — the storm comes from the system overhead and from where someone paved the marsh.Environment

7**The tide table.** Regulation-first and chronodiversity: read the table and go with it; every coast keeps its own clock.Systems

Four rungs in five spreads, in order, and the piece is the only zine on the site that prints the *downward* read as well — spread 6 walks Systems down to Environment down to Lived Experience down to attention, in the vocabulary of the ecosystemic model of distress.

**It has no Attention spread, and that is a real gap rather than a subtlety.** Monotropism enters No. 17 only by reference from the earlier zines. A piece about regulation that never reaches the floor of the ladder is running four rungs of a five-rung argument — which is worth noticing precisely because the ladder's own claim is that the floor is what holds the rest up.

### No. 15 · Every Creature's Own Sky — it descends before it climbs

2–3**Umwelt, and the senses beyond ours.** The tick's three signals; Nagel's bat. No view from nowhere — every creature's world, from the inside.Lived Experience

4**The cosmos looks back.** The star-stuff turn: the universe sees itself only from the inside, through the eyes it made.off-ladder · the turn

5**Minds have skies too.** Attention is a sense — it decides what shows up at all. Monotropic and polytropic as two skies, not a ranking.Attention

6**The gap goes both ways.** Milton's double empathy problem: when two umwelten meet, the failure belongs to neither.Relational

7**Mutual translation.** What the sky asks of us: curiosity rather than correction, and nobody owing you legibility.Environment

This one takes three spreads to reach the floor and then climbs three rungs in three. It arrives at Attention *from above* — the diagnostic direction — and then turns and builds upward from it. **That shape is not a flaw in the mapping; it is what a zine has to do.** A reader will not accept a claim about attention on spread two. They will accept it on spread five, once a tick and a bat have made the case that no two bodies share a world.

### No. 42 · Look for the Air — the whole thing reads downward

2–5**Aristotle to Apollo 15.** Falling explained by what a thing *is*, then character deleted from the explanation, then a hammer and a feather landing together in vacuum, on film.off-ladder · the chain

6**The hinge.** Deficit ideology is Aristotelian physics applied to children: the trajectory explained by an internal disposition, the situation absent.the diagnosis

7–10**Behaviourism, self-esteem, grit, the dashboard.** An industry that measures effort, focus and behaviour points, and has no field for the room.Systems

12**Where the air actually is.** Scarcity has a measurable cognitive cost — the medium, quantified, against the objection that only character is measurable.Lived Experience

13**When a feather falls slowly, look for the air.** The habit of mind, stated as an instruction.Environment

Systems, then the day, then the room. That is the diagnostic order, run without a word of the vocabulary, and it works because the physics has already taught the reader the move: *you would not design an intervention to build feather resilience.* Spread 11 is the chain's correction to itself over a misquotation, and sits outside this reading.

## Two that don't climb, and say so

This is the part that keeps the page honest. A framework you can find everywhere is a framework that has stopped saying anything.

[**No. 31, *Five Times a Crab, Seven Times Not***](https://starstuff.earth/five-times-a-crab-zine.html) occupies two rungs across ten spreads and states it in its own colophon: *“In Stimpunks' ARLES terms, this zine lives at the top two rungs — Environment and Systems.”* Convergence, attractors, Waddington's canalized landscape, the difference between a slope that was found and a wall that was poured. There is no ascent and there should not be. A zine about terrain has no business pretending to be about attention.

[**No. 16, *Minor Worlds***](https://starstuff.earth/minor-worlds-zine.html) runs the rungs badly out of order — Environment, Environment, Attention and Lived Experience together, Relational, Systems, Relational — and it is right to. Its claim is *about* the Environment rung: that the hum of the fluorescent light and the open-plan clatter are a built niche that got renamed the default. **You cannot argue that from below.** The rung under examination has to be on the table first.

And then the majority. Most of the numbered zines do not map onto this ladder at all, because most of them are not making a design argument — they are following a fact. [No. 9](https://starstuff.earth/the-lines-we-drew-zine.html) is about who holds the pen when a boundary gets drawn. [No. 61](https://starstuff.earth/thirteen-changes-zine.html) is about a ruler that would not travel one clade sideways. Those are arguments about measurement and authority, and forcing a rung onto them would tell you nothing about either.

## Four pieces already say it out loud

The rule at the top of this page is a rule going forward. It is not a description of the shelf, and the difference has to be published rather than smoothed over. Four pieces name the method on the page today:

- [**No. 17, *The Nearest Body***](https://starstuff.earth/the-nearest-body-zine.html) — spread 6's eyebrow reads *Ecosystemic distress · ARLES*, and the body spells out the downward read and links the glossary. Listed again in the colophon.
- [**No. 29, *Only in Relation***](https://starstuff.earth/only-in-relation-zine.html) — spread 6 *draws the ladder*, all five rungs, and argues from it: Relational sits near the base, and Regulation lives inside it, because regulation is not a private skill.
- [**No. 31, *Five Times a Crab***](https://starstuff.earth/five-times-a-crab-zine.html) — the two-rung placement in the colophon, plus the design-method line carried as an attributed quote.
- [**No. 59, *The Rest You Keep***](https://starstuff.earth/the-rest-you-keep-zine.html) — the colophon sentence quoted above, which is the rule stating itself.

Nothing here proposes stripping them. No. 29's ladder is doing real work on that spread — the whole point of the piece is that regulation lives in the relation, and the diagram is what makes the placement visible. And a collection with a [public error log](https://starstuff.earth/changelog.html) cannot quietly delete an exception in order to make a rule look cleaner than it is. **What the rule says is that a new zine does not become the fifth.**

Not

**Not a template.** ARLES is a design order and a diagnostic order. It is not a story shape, and a zine built to hit five rungs in five spreads would be a framework deliverable with a starfield behind it. No. 59 earns its ladder by paying for every rung with an animal.

**Not a grading scheme.** A one-rung zine is not a worse zine. [No. 31](https://starstuff.earth/five-times-a-crab-zine.html) sits at the top of the ladder and never moves, and it is one of the most carefully sourced pieces here.

**Not a claim about intent.** Four of the six maps above are retrospective, and a fifth (No. 58) is read off a proposal rather than a stated intent. Saying *this piece climbs the ladder* is not saying anyone was holding the ladder at the time, and the two are easy to blur once a diagram exists.

**Not a private vocabulary.** ARLES is published, in the open, and free to use with credit — [the glossary](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/arles/) and [the method](https://stimpunks.org/design/). It is kept off the spreads for the reader's sake, not for ours.

**Not evidence of anything.** A house framework found in house artifacts is the least surprising result available, and this page would be worthless as a defence of ARLES. It is a legibility aid: it explains why these zines keep arriving at the room instead of the person.
