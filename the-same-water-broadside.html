The Same
Water

Cool a glass of water and it does not slowly thicken toward solid. It stays liquid, liquid, liquid — and then, at a single temperature, it flips all at once into ice. Same molecules. Nothing added, nothing taken away.

Capacity is a state,
not a trait.

You are not inconsistent · you were near a boundary

Not a ramp — a cliff. One degree of difference, and the state changes.

The flip

A phase transition is a substance holding steady until a threshold in its conditions is crossed, then reorganising completely — with nothing added and nothing taken away.

The conditions

Whether water shows up as ice, liquid or steam depends on temperature and pressure — not on the water. There is no best state and no failed state.

The crossing

Right at a boundary a substance turns turbulent and hypersensitive, poised between two states. That is why the doorway is harder than the room.

Side B of B
What's actually happening
SupercoolingWater can be held below freezing and still liquid — until one small disturbance crystallises the whole glass at once. That's the meltdown after a day that “went fine”: not one trivial last straw, but a system kept past its limit.
Critical slowing downNear a transition, systems take far longer to settle after any knock. It isn't weakness that recovery swells near your edge — it's how systems behave there. Rest is the work, not the failure to do it.
Threshold anxietyThe dread that arrives before the crossing. For a monotropic mind a transition means tearing attention out of one deep channel and re-forming it in another — and done suddenly, or piled up, it can pull the mind past what it can hold.
Weather-bodiesAlways sensing and adjusting. Which makes the better question not how to make people more resilient to change, but how to make the climate more liveable.
What to ask for
Warning before a transition.The fix isn't a sturdier person. It's a better crossing.
A way back.A crossing you can reverse is one you can attempt.
Time at the crossing, not just at the task.The doorway is the expensive part. Budget for it.
Rest counted as the work.Regulation is not the absence of productivity. It's the thing that makes a next state possible.
The climate changed, not the person.If you want a different state, change the conditions — not the substance.
What not to say
“But you could do it yesterday.”However kindly meant, it lands as a charge of inconsistency — as if yesterday proved today is a choice.
“You just need more resilience.”It's a climate problem, and climates can be redesigned.
“It was only one small thing.”It was, and that's exactly how supercooling works.
Not:

Not broken — the same substance, in colder conditions.

Not regressing — re-adapting to a changed climate.

Not a resilience problem — a climate problem, and climates can be redesigned.

Ice isn't broken water — it's water somewhere colder. You aren't broken either: the same substance, meeting your conditions.

The physics is standard: phase transitions, supercooling and metastability, critical slowing down. The lived knowledge is ours and our kin's — Cynthia Kim on fluid adaptation (Musings of an Aspie) and Ombre Tarragnat on Autistic weather-bodies (Sextant, 2024), carried into Autistic experience by Helen Edgar (More Realms); both are idea attributions, not quotations. Threshold anxiety is Stimpunks'; the monotropic split is Tanya Adkin & David Gray-Hammond; monotropism is Murray, Lesser & Lawson (2005). If meltdown, shutdown or burnout are close to you right now, be gentle with yourself — rest is the work, and support is worth reaching for.
