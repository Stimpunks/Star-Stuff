The Shell Was

The egg crack moment is borrowed, true, and kinder than its own retelling. More than eighty per cent of a hatchling's calcium came from the wall it broke.

Two · the claim as received

Once the egg cracks,
there's no going back

There is a moment a great many of us recognise on description alone. Nothing new happens to you. Somebody says a sentence, or you read a list, and every year you have already lived rearranges itself behind you. The struggles were not personal failings. They were a nervous system doing what it does, in a world built to a different specification.

The therapist and author Essy Knopf — Autistic, ADHD, queer, disabled — set this out in March 2025, and named where the language comes from: the trans community, where getting your egg cracked is the moment somebody knows and accepts that they are trans. Knopf's argument is that Autistic and ADHD people have their own version of it.

Because once the egg cracks, once you see yourself for who you truly are, there's no going back — but that's a good thing.

Essy Knopf · The “Egg Crack” Moment: Realizing You're Neurodivergent · 2025

He is right about the feeling, and this zine is not an argument with him. The relief is real. So is the grief that arrives behind it, and the anger about the years, and he names all three.

The argument is with what happens to the metaphor after it leaves his mouth. Repeated enough, the egg cracks flattens into a door: one side undiagnosed and struggling, the other side self-aware and free, a threshold between them that you pass through once. It is a tidy story, and tidy stories travel.

So we did what this collection does. We went and looked at some eggs.

The incubator does not agree with the door. It agrees with something better.

Three · whose egg it was first

The word is
not ours

Before anything else, the debt. Egg, in this sense — a person who is trans and does not know it yet, an embryo of the person they will hatch into — is trans community language. We are borrowing it, and borrowed words get carried carefully or not at all.

Who said it first is not known, and that turns out to be the interesting part. Evelyn Bauer, writing for Them in December 2024, went looking. The earliest documented use is an Urban Dictionary entry for egg mode — “someone who is trans but doesn't know it yet” — posted by a user called bitplane7 on 23 August 2016. The subreddit r/egg_irl followed on 26 July 2017. Before that, nothing written down.

Bauer asked the historian Susan Stryker, who could not say when she first heard it, only that by 2019 it felt intuitively obvious. When the person who wrote the field's standard history cannot date a word, the word did not come from a book.

A word this good with no owner is not a gap in the record. It is what community language looks like from the inside.

Our own vocabulary works the same way. Neurodiversity has a first author and a contested one; much of the rest of it — the phrasings that actually get used, the ones that arrive already fitting — came out of mailing lists and comment threads and group chats, from people who were not building a canon and did not sign anything. The InLv list is where a great deal of it happened, and most of the individual sentences are unattributable now.

That is not sloppiness. It is the shape of a thing made by a community rather than issued to one. We say so here because the alternative — quietly letting a borrowed word sound like ours — is the small theft that the most-quotable-source failure is made of.

The borrowing also comes with a warning attached, and Bauer states it plainly. We keep it, and it returns on spread eight.

The Shell Was Your Calcium · Stimpunks 3
Four · what the shell actually does

The shell does not
get thinner

Here is the first thing the door story gets wrong, and it is worth the whole zine.

A hen's egg incubates for twenty-one days. From about day eleven, an extraembryonic membrane called the chorioallantoic membrane presses against the inside of the shell and begins dissolving it — pulling calcium out of the wall and passing it to the embryo. This is not damage. It is supply.

In 2022 a team at INRAE measured what that does to the shell. Halgrain and colleagues compared shells across incubation, and found something that sounds like a contradiction. Thickness between day 0 and day 15: no significant difference (p = 0.110). Breaking strength between day 11 and day 15: significantly reduced (p = 0.007).

Thickness, day 0 to day 15: no significant difference (p = 0.110)
Breaking strength, day 11 to day 15: significantly reduced (p = 0.007)
Halgrain et al., Frontiers in Physiology, 2022

The wall is being taken apart from the inside and it is not getting visibly thinner while it happens. Measure it and you would say nothing is going on. Weigh what it can bear and you would know everything is.

Nobody could see it.
There was nothing to see.

Every one of us who got a late answer has had some version of the same conversation. But you seemed fine. You did well at school. Nobody noticed anything. And the honest reply has always been that there was nothing to notice, because holding it together is the thing that was happening. The masking was load-bearing right up to the week it wasn't.

The shell agrees. Intact, correct, full thickness, and by day fifteen it will fail under a load it carried easily on day one.

Five · where the shell went

Into the skeleton

So the calcium comes out of the wall. The obvious next question is the one the metaphor never asks, because the metaphor is busy with the door: where does it go?

The eggshell supplies more than eighty per cent of all the calcium a chick embryo gets — roughly 2.2 grams per egg — and in altricial birds, the ones that hatch helpless, more than ninety. Between days eleven and fifteen the embryo's skeleton holds more than eighty per cent of all the calcium in the animal, and at hatching it holds about three quarters.

Put those two sentences next to each other and read them again.

>80% of the embryo's calcium comes from the shell · at hatch the skeleton holds ~75% of it
Halgrain et al., 2022

The container becomes the frame. Not metaphorically — by mass. The thing that held the animal in is, by the time the animal leaves, most of what holds the animal up. There is no moment where the shell is discarded and the skeleton begins. They are the same calcium at two addresses.

And that calcium has a longer history than the egg. Every one of those atoms was assembled inside a star that is no longer there — fused in the shells of a massive one, scattered when it failed. It has been a shell before. It has been seawater and limestone and somebody's tooth.

Then it becomes bone, and bone does the thing this whole site is named for. Press on it and it answers in current: collagen is piezoelectric, as Iwao Yasuda showed in the 1950s and Bassett and colleagues confirmed after him. Load a bone and it generates a charge, and the charge is part of how it decides where to grow.

The wall you broke is now the part of you that turns pressure into signal.

You did not escape the shell.
You stood up in it.

Six · the messy middle

Breathing, and still
inside

This spread is for whoever is reading it and does not know yet.

At the blunt end of an egg there is a pocket of air, the air cell, which grows through incubation as water evaporates out through the shell's pores. Near the end, the embryo pushes its beak through the inner membrane into that pocket. This is called internal pipping, and it is the moment the lungs go to work — respiration transfers from the chorioallantoic membrane, which has been doing the gas exchange all along, to the animal's own airways.

Margaret Vince traced how that changeover happens in the domestic fowl, and found it is not one event but three: the major airways aerate, then respiratory movements begin, then the finest branches aerate slowly (Vince, Journal of Physiology, 1976). A staged, unhurried handover.

All of it happens before the shell is broken.

Internal pipping · the lungs start before the shell does
Vince, Journal of Physiology, 1976

Set that egg in front of an observer and it is an egg. Whole, unmarked, apparently nothing happening. Inside it is an animal that has already started breathing air and has not broken anything yet.

The messy middle is a
respiratory stage.

There is a period — hours, in a hen — where you are doing the new thing and still entirely inside the old container, and from outside there is no evidence whatsoever. Not hesitation. Not failing to commit. Not being a bit Autistic, or trying on a label, or whatever else gets said to people who are mid-changeover and cannot yet produce a certificate.

It is a stage with a name, and everything that hatches goes through it.

Seven · the clicking

You crack when you hear
others cracking

A clutch of quail eggs is laid over days but hatches together, near enough. For a long time this was filed as one of those things.

Margaret Vince, working in Cambridge in the 1960s, put instruments on the eggs and found out why. In the last days before hatching the embryos click — an audible, intermittent sound in time with their breathing — and the clicking is not incidental. It is a signal, and the neighbours act on it.

An egg placed in a clutch a full day behind the others will speed itself up and hatch with them (Vince, 1964). Development accelerates, on the strength of hearing other embryos further along than itself.

And then the part that matters most here. Vince went looking for the other direction, and found it: a slower click rate — around 0.9 clicks per second — retards embryos that are ahead (Vince, 1968; 1984). Synchronisation is not the stragglers being dragged up to standard. It runs both ways.

Bidirectional synchronisation · the behind accelerate, the ahead are retarded
Vince, Animal Behaviour 1964, 1968 · J. Exp. Zoology 1984

We keep saying that community is what makes this survivable, and we usually mean it as encouragement. In an incubator it is not encouragement. It is the mechanism. The clicking is how the clutch hatches together — remove it and they don't.

Which is a fair description of how most people's egg actually cracks. Almost nobody works it out alone in a room. It is a video, a thread, a friend saying have you ever looked into this, somebody one step further along making a noise where you could hear it. You were not given information. You were given a rate.

The ones ahead
slow down and wait.

That is the finding we would least have predicted and like the most. Not everyone hurrying to catch the fastest embryo. A clutch arriving at the same time because it is worth more to arrive together, and the cost of that is paid by whoever was ahead.

The Arrau turtle does its own version of this, calling from inside the shell and going on calling in the river; it is drawn in The Hatchery and its story there is about who was listening.

Eight · the refusals

What this zine
is not

An egg is a good enough image that it will carry almost anything, including several things it should not. So, plainly:

Not: cracking somebody else's egg

Bauer's warning, kept as given: naming somebody an egg — especially in a gossipy, pejorative or confrontational way, and most of all to their face — can push a person further into the closet, not out of it. The biology says the same thing with no ambiguity at all. The egg tooth is on the inside of the shell. Nobody pips from outside; an egg opened from without is not a hatching, it is an egg somebody broke. You cannot do this for a person. You can only click.

Not: the person before was fake

The tidiest and most damaging reading — that there was an impostor and now there is the real one. The shell was your calcium. It is in your legs. Every year spent holding it together was spent by you, in a body that was already this body, and none of it was somebody else's life.

Not: a graduation

Mair and colleagues read 225 posts about late identification and one of the four themes they came back with is post-diagnosis burnout. Hatching is not the end of the hard part; in a good deal of the testimony it is where a particular hard part starts, generally with no support attached. Anyone selling the crack as an arrival has not read the follow-up.

Not: permission from nature

Chick embryos do not have identities, or closets, or a diagnosis waiting at twenty-nine. Nothing in an incubator authorises anything about us, and a universe that could approve could also decline — we would not take the verdict either way. This is a rhyme, and rhymes are for holding a thing in mind, not for proving it. Down to is not because of.

Not: the egg as the enemy

The container gets cast as the villain because the story needs one, and it will not hold. That shell kept an animal at the right temperature and humidity for three weeks, exchanged its gases through its own pores, and then handed over most of its substance. Masking is like this too, which is the uncomfortable part: it cost us enormously and it also got some of us here alive. You are allowed to be angry about a thing that worked.

What the science does kill is narrow and holds well. It kills the door — the idea that this is one threshold, crossed once, with a settled person on the far side. Nothing about hatching is a threshold. It is a supply chain, a staged handover of breathing, a negotiated arrival time, and a tool you grow and then lose.

Nine · not a door

Not a door.
A cycle

In 2026 Ally Pax Arcari Mair and colleagues at Edinburgh went looking for what actually happens to people after a late identification. They gathered 731 public posts about grief and neurodivergence across seven platforms, kept the 225 that spoke to grief and relief in relation to identifying late, and read them.

They did not find a door. They found a process, which they named the Grief, Relief, and Belief Cycle — and they were explicit that it is oscillating, non-linear, and different for everyone. Four themes sit inside it:

Theme one

The Life I Could Have Had — the loss and the regret. The jobs, the friendships, the school years that would have gone another way.

Theme two

Grieving for My Younger Self — looking back at the child who was called lazy and difficult, now knowing what was actually happening to them.

Theme three

Feeling Gratitude — the relief, and the self-understanding it buys. This is the part the popular version keeps.

Theme four

Post-Diagnosis Burnout — the emotional and physical toll, and the absence of anything waiting to help. This is the part the popular version drops.

A cycle means it comes round again. The gratitude does not retire the grief; you can be four years into knowing and get flattened by the life you could have had, on a Tuesday, for no reason. That is not you failing at having hatched. That is the documented shape of the thing.

And the authors are blunt about who should be doing something with that. Their recommendation is a paradigm shift in which identification is treated as a first step, rather than a terminal component of a service — wraparound support before, during and beyond. The cycle is not a personal weather system to be endured privately. It is a care gap with a citation.

Diagnosis is a first step, not a terminal component.

Mair, Gonzalez-Figueroa, McConachie, Goodall & Gillespie-Smith · Autism · 2026

One last thing about hatching, for whoever needs it.

The tool that does the breaking is temporary. Birds grow an egg tooth, a small hard point of keratin on the beak, purely to score the shell from inside; turtles and crocodilians grow a caruncle on the snout, lizards and snakes a true tooth, and even the echidna — a mammal, hatching from an egg — has both. It evolved over and over, because getting out is that important. Behind it works the musculus complexus, a neck muscle that swells specifically for this job.

And afterwards: the egg tooth drops off, or is resorbed, within days. The hatching muscle shrinks. The stereotyped thrusting movements stop and never return. None of that apparatus is part of the animal's ordinary life.

The one who did the cracking
was built for the cracking.

Whoever you had to be to get out of that shell — the one who read everything at 3 a.m., who rehearsed the sentence for a fortnight, who was furious for a year — was the right animal for that fortnight. They were also grown for one job, and they are allowed to be put down. Mair's second theme is grief for a younger self, and this is the near end of it: you can mourn the version of you who did the hard part, and still not need them any more.

You are not the shell and you are not out of it either. You are what the shell became, standing in a wider room, still made of the same star.

Love you down to your star stuff.

Credit where it is owed

Egg and getting your egg cracked, in the sense used throughout, are trans community language, and we are borrowing them. No individual coined them so far as anyone has been able to establish: Evelyn Bauer's piece for Them traces the earliest documented use to an Urban Dictionary entry for egg mode by the user bitplane7 on 23 August 2016, and r/egg_irl to 26 July 2017, and records Susan Stryker being unable to date her own first encounter with it. We state the origin as unknown rather than picking the most citable name, which is the failure Too Good to Check catalogues. The extension of the term to Autistic and ADHD experience on spread 2 is Essy Knopf's, from his own video, and he credits the trans origin himself rather than presenting it as his own — we note that because plenty of retellings do not. The Grief, Relief, and Belief Cycle and its four themes are Mair, Gonzalez-Figueroa, McConachie, Goodall and Gillespie-Smith's, named by them, and the phrase a first step, rather than a terminal component of a service is theirs. Piezoelectric bone is Iwao Yasuda's finding, with C. Andrew L. Bassett and colleagues confirming and extending it. The hatching-synchrony work is Margaret A. Vince's, and the reading of the retarding direction as mutual aid is ours, not hers.

What we checked, and what we did not

Halgrain et al. (2022) is open access and was read at source; both figures on spread 4 are the paper's own — thickness between EID0 and EID15 not significantly different at p = 0.110, breaking strength between EID11 and EID15 significantly reduced at p = 0.007 — as are the calcium proportions and the ~2.21 g figure on spread 5. Mair et al. (2026) was read in full from the published article. Vince (1976) was read at abstract level; the three-stage lung aeration sequence and the beak-clapping observation are quoted from that abstract and not from the full text. The duration of internal pipping is deliberately not given as a number. Incubation and husbandry sources widely quote twelve to twenty-four hours between internal and external pipping in domestic fowl, and it may well be right, but we could not confirm it against a primary we were able to open — Visschedijk (1968) is paywalled at every route we tried — so spread 6 says hours and stops there. Vince's 1964, 1968 and 1984 papers are all behind publisher paywalls; the acceleration result, the 24-hour catch-up, the retardation direction and the 0.9 clicks-per-second figure reach us from the abstracts, the titles (one of which is literally Retardation as a factor in the synchronization of hatching) and contemporaneous reporting, and are stated here as well-established rather than as figures we verified in the papers themselves. The egg tooth and musculus complexus material is given as standard comparative anatomy — its independent evolution across birds, squamates, turtles, crocodilians and monotremes is well documented, and the echidna case has a recent primary — with no precise resorption timing printed, because that varies by species. Knopf's words on spread 2 are transcribed from his video; the transcript we worked from renders his name as “Essie”, which is an auto-captioning artefact, and the spelling here is taken from the channel's own metadata. The parallel between masking and the shell, the reading of internal pipping as the messy middle, and the sort of this material into five corrections are all ours, arrived at after the sources and not before them.

The fact here is that an eggshell loses most of its calcium to the skeleton of the animal it contains, without measurably thinning while it happens. The belonging claim is not that hatching approves of us.
