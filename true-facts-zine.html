The credit

A comedy channel in the ancestry

Six of the zines here and three of the field guides exist because of a YouTube series about animals with jokes in it. Until this page, its author's name appeared nowhere in this project at all.

Ze Frank makes True Facts — nature documentary footage, narrated straight into absurdity, about echidnas and pangolins and anglerfish and mantis shrimp. Ryan watched it with a young person in our community whose special interest is sharks, now studying to be a marine biologist. Out of that watching came No. 32, No. 34 and Field Guide No. 9, which already say who they were written for — and behind them the whole Kin collection, which is the citation-dense natural history end of this site.

A shark catalogue, a Greenland shark's 156-year childhood, a skeleton that skipped bone. None of those get written by someone who was not already watching animal videos with somebody they love.where Kin actually comes from

So why was the credit missing? Because it does not look like a source. We are careful to the point of tedium about citing papers, and there is a whole page — Cosmic Connections — arguing that nothing here comes from nowhere. But the thing that made us want to write about sharks was not a paper. It was a bloke doing a voice over some footage of a fish, and that kind of debt slips through, because the citation habit is pointed at claims rather than at causes.

This page is the correction. It is filed as an easter egg rather than on the front page for one honest reason, which spread seven deals with directly: the tone does not port. That is not a criticism, and it is not a hedge either. It is the actual situation, and saying it plainly is more respectful than a credit that pretends we are doing the same thing.

Where the joke lands

The animal is never the punchline

What we took from True Facts is not that it makes science fun. Plenty of things make science fun. It is something narrower and much harder, and it took us a while to see it.

Watch where the comedy actually points. It lands on the narrator — his own bafflement, his own voice, his willingness to be the idiot in the room. It lands on human expectation — what we assumed an animal was for. It lands on language — the ridiculous names we gave things. And in our reading it does not land, at any point, on the organism for being a defective version of something else.

That is the same move this site makes, arrived at from the opposite end. Every field guide here has a “sounds like / actually” table, and its whole job is to catch a verdict somebody already handed down — the failed brown dwarf, the primitive shark, the simple nervous system — and take it back.

Our tables correct a verdict. True Facts just never issues one. It is the same destination and it arrives without needing the apparatus, which is a slightly annoying thing to discover about your own method.the honest version of the comparison

And it is not that the animals are treated reverently — they absolutely are not. They get called ridiculous, they get compared to household objects, the delivery is relentless. But ridiculous is not the same as deficient. You can find a creature hilarious and still describe, accurately and at length, exactly what it is extremely good at. The two things sit together the whole way through, which is the part worth stealing.

Stated as a reading, not a finding. This is our interpretation of a body of work, not a claim we can measure. Somebody could go through the catalogue and argue the opposite. We think the pattern holds; we are not pretending it is a result.

His own words

Flawed and wonderful

Asked what he is doing, in an interview with Pamela Hill Nettleton for U.S. Catholic in 2021, Ze Frank gave an answer that could be lifted straight onto this site's masthead.

I like reminding people that we are flawed and wonderful. I like helping people feel less alone.Ze Frank, interviewed by Pamela Hill Nettleton · U.S. Catholic 86:7, July 2021

Flawed and wonderful, in the same breath, with no ranking between them. Not flawed but wonderful, which would make the wonder a consolation. Not wonderful despite. Both at once, as a single description — which is very close to what the neurodiversity paradigm says about human variation, and it arrived here from a man narrating footage of a sea slug.

He is not making a claim about neurodiversity, and we are not putting one in his mouth. He is talking about people watching videos on the internet. But the shape of the sentence is the shape of the argument, and this collection is built on noticing when a shape turns up somewhere it was not aimed.

Nettleton's own framing in that piece is the sharpest thing written about him. Her words, not his:

Frank is a kind of Mister Rogers of the internet for grown-ups. He makes good stuff that makes people remember how good they are too.Pamela Hill Nettleton · U.S. Catholic, 2021 — her characterisation, not his

And she catches the thing about the series that most coverage misses: it honours scientists — people generally ignored by most media — and the arcane subjects they spend their lives on. Which turns out to be literally true of the production, not just the vibe. That is the next spread.

The unusual direction

It got more rigorous, not less

Here is the part that made us pay proper attention, because it runs against the way everything on the internet normally goes.

The early videos were jokes with animals in them. Sheela Clary, profiling him for SUCCESS in 2022, is blunt about the first one: it “included neither truth nor facts.” Spliced clips, a voice, a punchline. That is a format that found an audience — and the ordinary next move, the one the incentives all point at, is to make more of exactly that, faster.

He went the other way. He left BuzzFeed by April 2019 and gave the series his full attention, and what happened then is the interesting bit:

what changed as it went full-time
thenafter
spliced found clipsfootage from the NOAA public-domain archive, and from scientists directly
shortlonger, and better-researched
jokes about animalsnamed researchers thanked on the work

His own account of it, to Clary: “I was much more interested in the science part.” The videos got longer and better-researched as his relationship to the scientific community changed — and the thanks now carry actual names: Robert E. Fuller, Dr Angela Horner, Dr Natalie Uomini, Dr Anthony Friscia, Dr Chris J. Law, Dr Karol Zub, Dr Tom Glass, Evan Buechley.

A comedy channel that discovered it liked the citations. We are not going to pretend that isn't the same thing that happened to us.the arc, recognised

Because it is. This site started with a poem about bones and now maintains a public error log, a six-part taxonomy of how facts go wrong, and five automated gates. Nobody plans that. You just keep finding out the real thing is more interesting than the version you were going to make up, and the apparatus accumulates behind you.

Nobody checked

True facts about True Facts

This site is fairly insufferable about going to the primary source. So when we went to write down when True Facts started, we did the thing we always do, and the answer came back three different ways.

when did it begin?
sourcesays
SUCCESS, 2022first installment 2013; Hedgehogs was “his first stab at it”
Wikipediabegan December 2012, with Baby Echidnas
the datesa hedgehog piece was being blogged 16 Nov 2012; the echidna is 7 Dec 2012; and a hedgehog Redo is April 2013

So the 2022 profile is right about the animal and, by the contemporaneous record, a year out on the date — and there is an obvious candidate for how.

Contested Our reading: the Redo is the culprit. There is an April 2013 True Facts about the Hedgehog (Redo), and a profile written nine years later, working from a catalogue where the redo is the surviving copy, would land on exactly this answer — right animal, wrong year. That is an inference from upload dates, not something anybody told us, and it is marked rather than asserted.

Which is not a gotcha, and we would rather it did not read as one. This is the ordinary way a story goes smooth — a detail gets rounded, the tidier version is the one that travels, and nobody involved did anything wrong. We have published worse. There is a whole page of ours, dated, where a line got credited to Baldwin that is Dr King's, and a fungal network got asserted because it flattered a metaphor.

The pleasure is just that the series is called True Facts. Its own founding date turns out to be a true-facts problem, and the fix is the fix it would use on itself: go and look at what is actually on the tape.

Different room

We did not take the register

And now the reason this is an easter egg instead of a front-page piece, said straight, because a credit that dodges it is worth less than no credit.

True Facts is bawdy. The innuendo is relentless and frequently the entire point, the voice is doing a bit, and a great deal of the humour is about mating, genitals and appetite delivered in the register of a man who cannot believe what he is looking at. It is very funny and it is not remotely what we sound like.

The reason the register does not port is not squeamishness. It is who is in the room. This site is read by people who have been laughed at — often about their bodies, often about the noises they make, often by someone who then explained it was affectionate. A tone that lands as warm generosity in one room lands, in another, as the opening of something that has happened before.

Same joke, same goodwill, different room. The register is not a property of the words; it is a property of who has been on the receiving end of them.why the tone stays where it is

So this is not disapproval, and we want that on the record. His register is doing real work that ours cannot: it buys fifteen straight minutes of actual biology from an audience that would not sit through a field guide. That is a genuine access achievement and it is not a lesser one than ours. It is a different door into the same building.

What we took was the mechanism, not the voice. Ridiculous is not deficient. You can hold something up to the light, describe precisely what it does, find it absurd and marvellous at once, and never once suggest it should have been something else. That works in his register. It works in ours. It is the part that survives translation.

Not:an endorsement, a collaboration, or anything he agreed to. Ze Frank has no involvement in this page, was not asked, and has not seen it. Every reading here is ours and so is every error.
Not:a claim that he is arguing about neurodiversity. He is making videos about animals. We are the ones setting his work beside ours and noticing the shape.
Not:a gotcha about the dates. The origin muddle is the most ordinary thing in publishing, we have done worse and logged it, and spread six says so before it says anything else.
Not:disapproval of the humour. Different room, different job. The crude register is the thing buying a general audience a quarter-hour of real zoology, which is more than most science communication manages.
Not:“science is only good when it is funny.” Accessible and entertaining are different axes. Plenty of us need the quiet version with the citations in it, and that is not a failure of humour.
Not:a claim that comedy is the way in for everybody. For some of us the jokes are the barrier and the field notes are the door. Both doors, always.
Not:an origin story for this site. One influence among many, and Cosmic Connections holds that argument. Nothing here comes from nowhere, and nothing here comes from one place either.

What this page is, and is not

It is a credit and an appreciation, written entirely by us, quoting published interviews with attribution. Ze Frank has no involvement, was not consulted, and has not seen it. Nothing here should be read as partnership, endorsement, or his agreement with a single word of it — including the reading on spread three, which is our interpretation of his work and not a claim about his intentions.

It sits in Easter Eggs rather than on the front page because spread seven is true: the tone does not port, and pretending otherwise would flatter both of us. An egg here is not a lower standard — same sourcing, same grading, same ledger row, same gates. It is just not in the grid.

A rhyme, not a proof

A man narrating footage of a sea slug is not evidence for a paradigm of human variation. Where this page sets his sentence beside ours, it is noticing that the same shape turns up in both — which is worth saying, and is not an argument. The debt on spread two, though, is not a rhyme. That one is just true, and it was overdue.
