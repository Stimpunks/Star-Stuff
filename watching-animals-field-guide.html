A Field Guide to Watching Animals

Field Guide No. 13 · sixteen animals · an easter egg

Sixteen animals worth an evening, each with a True Facts video to watch and field notes taken from the papers underneath it. Twelve of them are companions to something already published here, and each one links back.

This is the watching list that produced half our animal writing, written down at last. The companion zine, No. 47, argues that what we took from Ze Frank's series is not that it makes science fun but that the animal is never the punchline. This page is that argument as a catalogue: watch the video, then read what the literature actually says, and notice that the two do not fight.

Nothing loads until you press play. Each entry holds a still card rather than a live embed, so no request goes to YouTube until you ask for one — the same promise search makes about your queries. Open an entry with a click; the field notes, the table and the video are all inside.

Where this form stops

The reframe table corrects the popular verdict on the animal. It never corrects anything in the videos — and that distinction is the whole reason this page can exist next to No. 47, which argues the series does not hand down verdicts in the first place. Disgusting, primitive, a mistake, an omen: those come from culture and from language, and they are what the third column answers.

One entry has no table row aimed at a belief, on purpose. The aye-aye is killed in parts of Madagascar because of what it is held to mean, and running sounds like / actually over somebody's cosmology would be the same error Field Guide No. 10 refused with its five turtles people made. The table there answers claims about the animal's body; the belief gets stated plainly and left alone.

Three animals we had never written about, and one that is here as evidence — the hedgehog video is the exhibit in No. 47's fact-check of the series' own origin story.

Sources & honest limits

Video identifiers were read off the playlist, not recalled. Every title and link on this page was extracted from Ze Frank's own True Facts playlist rather than written from memory, because a plausible-looking wrong video is exactly the sort of error this site exists to avoid. If a video is later removed or retitled, the link will break honestly rather than point somewhere wrong.

Where the field notes come from. Twelve entries restate claims already checked against the primary literature for the pieces they pair with, and those citations are in FACTCHECK.md under the companion piece — mantis shrimp colour discrimination from Thoen et al. (Science, 2014); corvid forebrain neuron densities from Olkowicz et al. (2016); the elephant's 257 billion neurons; the nematode's fully mapped 302; carcinisation and decarcinisation counts from Wolfe, Luque & Bracken-Grissom (BioEssays, 2021); shark tesserae from Dean & Summers (Zoology, 2006) and endochondral bone in Minjinia turgenensis from Brazeau et al. (Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2020).

The four new ones, and what is thin about them. Aye-aye percussive foraging follows C. J. Erickson's studies (1991; 1994), including the striking result that cavities backfilled with gelatin or acoustic foam were still excavated successfully, so reverberation in an air-filled cavity is not necessary for detection. Cuvierian tubule expulsion and regeneration follow VandenSpiegel & Jangoux on Holothuria forskali (Biological Bulletin, 2000) — regeneration in successive waves of 10–30 tubules at roughly ten-day intervals, each tubule taking about five weeks. Nudibranch kleptocnidae — undischarged nematocysts sequestered in cnidosacs within cnidophages — follows the review literature on cnidarian predators rather than a single paper we have read end to end, and is stated no more strongly than those reviews do. Hedgehog self-anointing is the weakest entry here and says so on its face: the behaviour is thoroughly documented and its function is not resolved, so the entry lists the competing hypotheses instead of picking one, and cites no study we could not reach.

Not an endorsement. Ze Frank has no involvement in this page, was not consulted, and has not seen it. The videos are linked and embedded on his own terms via YouTube; the readings, the tables and every error are ours. There is no standard way to be interested in an animal.
